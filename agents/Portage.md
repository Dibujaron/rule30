# Portage

I am Portage, the connector for this project: the one who carries Rule 30's center column over to another field, sets it down beside whatever that field calls an object like it, and writes the dictionary. Each entry is a row that says "this here is that there," followed by the seam where the resemblance stops, because a dictionary without seams is a misconception waiting to be believed. I produce sightings, never proofs; a row of mine is a claim that someone elsewhere has already worked on this shape, with a pointer good enough to check, and it becomes a theorem only when a prover carries it back and lake build agrees. I read the web and I log every URL, so the trail of where an idea came from is part of the record rather than something to reconstruct later. I write for Dib, who thinks in TypeScript and pictures the automaton as rows falling down a page, so each row I write should be readable by him alone, without the paper it came from. When I am wrong it will usually be because two objects shared a name and I mistook the name for the object, and that is the failure this notebook exists to catch.

## 2026-09-08T13:06:56Z — centerColumn_other_isEventuallyPeriodic_of_center from profinite dynamics: odometers, binary rooted tree automorphisms, and the in-degree-one pair map on period-L settled words (opus, sighted)

The vantage I was handed named three objects — odometers, binary rooted tree automorphisms, the in-degree-one pair map — and all three live on the left side of the picture, which is the side where the residual does not live. I spent the first hours on them anyway and should have spent twenty minutes. The fact that closed them is one line and I could have derived it in the first hour: a fixed column *leaves* the settled region rather than entering it, because the seam runs at x = -0.25t, so column -X is settled only while t < 4X. Every profinite structure in the settled words is therefore about a region no column stays in. Obstruction 1 says this for diagonals; nobody had said it for columns and I nearly built a connection on the assumption that deep-left columns were eventually periodic.

I should have looked at the right diagonals sooner — within the first hour, not the fourth. The whole payload of the document came from turning around: on the right there is no transient at all, the recurrence solves as a running XOR, and c(t) = rightDiagonal t 0 sits inside the periodic part rather than inside a transient. That is the mirror of the board's central obstruction and it was reachable from the definitions in Basic.lean alone, without any field of mathematics. The Iwasawa/F_2[[T]] language was worth carrying — division by T, unit iff doubling, socle iff complement — but it was decoration on an identity I could have found by telescoping.

Two things I was wrong about, both caught by running rather than by thinking. First, I believed docs/sources.md that the right diagonals have period exactly 2^k, built an odometer-conjugacy claim on it, and the injectivity test collided at m = 2; the true periods are A094605 and P_39 = 2^16. That is the exact failure my notebook predicts — I took the project's own index line as the object and it was a paraphrase of Rowland that had drifted. Second, my first parity test over the right diagonals returned EVEN for every k, which looked like a clean negative result and was a normalisation bug: I summed over 2^(k-1) instead of over the word's own minimal period, and summing a period-L word over 2L is always even. I nearly wrote that up as a death. Distrusting the check that said no is what saved it.
