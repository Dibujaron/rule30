# Sighting: the left damage front as a growth process in a fixed environment

**Vantage.** Percolation and first-passage growth of the damage front: Eden
clusters, the KPZ class, interacting particle systems, and whether a
deterministic-background front admits a speed bound below 1.

**Connector.** Rosetta, 2026-09-09. Nothing here is a proof. A connection is a
sighting: a direction for a prover to walk, and a dictionary whose rows can be
checked one at a time. Scripts are under `explorer/rosetta_*.mjs`; every number
below says which one produced it and at what depth.

---

## 1. The problem, seen from outside

An infinite row of cells, each black or white, indexed by the integers, is
updated in lock-step: a cell's new colour is a fixed function of its own colour
and its two neighbours' — here, *left* XOR (*centre* OR *right*). Start from one
black cell in an otherwise white row and draw the successive rows down the page.
Now read one bit per row: the colour of the cell directly below the seed. Is
that infinite binary sequence eventually periodic? Nothing in it repeats to any
depth anyone has computed, and no proof exists. (The project's board states the
question in an
implication form — "if the centre column repeats, some other column repeats" —
but a proved theorem of the board says no two distinct columns can both repeat,
so the hypothesis and conclusion are incompatible and the implication is
logically equivalent to "the centre column does not repeat". There is no weaker
statement hiding in it.)

Restated in the vantage's own terms. Run two copies of the same deterministic
growth process on a line and let them differ somewhere; the set of cells where
they disagree is a cluster growing downward in time. Its right edge advances
exactly one cell per row, always. Its left edge `F(t)` obeys an exact local law:
it advances one cell to the left when the cell immediately left of it — a cell
the two copies agree on, hence part of a *fixed background* — is white, and
otherwise it stays put or jumps back to the right by any amount. Measured over
159,999 rows (`explorer/maskfront.mjs`) for the pair that matters here, the seed's
picture against the picture of its own settled region: 59.04% advance, 14.83%
stay, 26.12% retreat, retreats averaging 1.30 cells, net drift −0.2497 cells per
row. The background is not
random and not stationary: it is one fixed array, whose diagonals are periodic
sequences of power-of-two period, generated by a recurrence that never repeats a
state. The question: **does this front admit a linear speed bound below 1 — and
in particular below 1/2, which is what the project's onset wall
`leftDiagonal_onset_le` needs?** Over arbitrary pairs of configurations the
answer is no: there are pairs whose left front moves at exactly 1 (crystals A3).
So the whole question is whether *this* background buys anything.

The answer this document reaches: **yes, it buys a bound, and the bound is
0.50106 — which is on the wrong side of 1/2 by one part in a thousand.**

| Ergodic optimisation, maximising Birkhoff averages (Jenkinson) | `λ₋` is the maximal white density over admissible paths | maximising measures are typically supported on periodic orbits; the orbit driving this environment (adjacent pairs of settled words) is proved never to repeat |

| Interface depinning in a disordered medium | black settled cells are pinning centres; `λ₋` is the depinning velocity | the depinning transition sits at `v = 0`; ours sits at `v = 1` (free flight along a white channel) and everything else is "pinned", so the order parameter is on the wrong end |
| Run-length-limited (RLL) constrained coding | the longest white run of a settled word is the `k` of a `(d,k)` constraint | the constraint is not uniform: the longest white run grows with the period (29 at diagonal 823,345), and the periods are unbounded |

| Resonance capture (KAM, small divisors) — the unlikely entry | the eventually-white diagonals are resonances the trajectory must not be captured by | there is no Hamiltonian and no small divisor; what survives is only the shape of the question, and the capture-avoidance here is already a theorem (every diagonal settles) |
| Kingman's subadditive ergodic theorem | `λ₋` is the limit of `F(t)/t` | **nobody has proved that limit exists.** Every number in this document is a measurement of a finite window of one deterministic sequence |

## 3. Connections

Common notation for all five. `S` is the settled picture (crystal 47: the rule 30
evolution of the settled row, whose diagonals are the periodic words `S_k`);
`E = picture xor S` is the transient band; `F(t) = min { x : E(t, x) = 1 }` is its
left front; `κ(t) = t + F(t)` is the diagonal the front sits on, so that the cell
immediately left of the front, `(t, F(t) − 1)`, is the settled word `S_{κ−1}` read
at index `1 − F(t)`. Crystals A2 gives the law exactly: **the front advances one
cell iff that settled cell is white**, and otherwise stays or retreats.

### 3.1 The front is the edge of a cluster of *dying* paths, not a waiting walker — and the whole of the missing speed is in the dying

**The claim.** Everything the settled background can contribute to the left
front's speed is captured by a single never-retreating comparison walker whose
speed is measurably **0.50106**; the real front's 0.2497 is bought entirely by the
fact that damage paths *die* rather than wait — so `leftDiagonal_onset_le` is a
survival statement about the band, not a passage-time statement about the
background, and it misses being provable from the background by one part in a
thousand.

Define `G` by the background alone:

```
G(t+1) = G(t) − 1   if  S_{t+G(t)−1}(1 − G(t)) = false,
G(t+1) = G(t)       otherwise.
```

`G` never looks at the picture. `G(t) ≤ F(t)` for all `t` if they start together,
by a two-case induction: if `G(t) < F(t)` then `G(t+1) ≤ G(t) ≤ F(t) − 1 ≤ F(t+1)`
because `F` never advances by more than one; if `G(t) = F(t)` they read the same
cell, and either both advance or `G` stays while `F` stays or retreats. So `G` is
to the left of `F` at every row, and `G`'s speed is an upper bound for `F`'s.

The induction uses nothing about `F` except the local law, so it proves more than
a comparison: **`G` is optimal.** Any trajectory at all that advances only through
white settled cells and otherwise fails to advance is weakly to the right of `G`
at every row — a retreat can never pay off later, because the moment a trajectory
falls behind `G` the first case pins it there for ever. So 0.50106 is not the
bound that one particular comparison happens to give; it is the exact supremum of
what the local law and the settled background *together* permit, and no argument
using only those two ingredients can prove anything smaller.

**The dictionary.**

| Here | First-passage / oriented percolation |
|---|---|
| a row of the picture, time running down | one time step of the growth process |
| the settled picture `S` left of the seam | the fixed environment: white cells are open sites, black cells closed |
| the transient band `E` | the open cluster of damage, growing downward |
| the band's right edge | the trivial side: advances at exactly 1 every row (left-permutivity) |
| the front `F(t)` | the leftmost point `r_t` of the cluster |
| crystals A2 | an oriented path may step left only through an open site |
| a stall, 15% of rows | the leading path is blocked this row |
| a retreat, 26% of rows averaging 1.30 cells | the leading path **dies** and the edge falls back to the next surviving one |
| `λ₋` | the edge speed `α` |
| the wall `leftDiagonal_onset_le` | `α ≤ 1/2` |
| crystals A3, worst case exactly 1 | `α(p) = 1` at `p = 1`: with every site open, the edge is free |
| the never-retreating walker `G` | a walker that *waits* at a closed site instead of dying |
| **Seam 1** | there is no measure. The environment is one fixed array; Kingman's subadditive theorem needs a stationary one, and the only pair-uniform bound available is speed 1 — so **crystals A3 is precisely the statement that the subadditive route returns nothing**, not an accident of what has been tried |
| **Seam 2** | the field's intuition runs backwards here. In percolation, blocking is what slows the edge; here blocking (waiting) gives 0.501 and it is *dying* that gives 0.250. A theorist reaching for "the background blocks the front" will prove the wrong half |
| **Seam 3** | the environment contains genuinely infinite open oriented paths, which no percolation model at `p < 1` has (§3.2) |

**What it leans on.**
[van den Berg & Kesten, *Inequalities for the Time Constant in First-Passage
Percolation*, Ann. Appl. Probab. 3 (1993)](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-applied-probability/volume-3/issue-1/Inequalities-for-the-Time-Constant-in-First-Passage-Percolation/10.1214/aoap/1177005507.full),
fetched: "the shortest travel time from (0, 0,…, 0) to (n, 0, …, 0) is
asymptotically equal to nμ, for some constant μ, which is called the time
constant", and "If F and G are distribution functions with F ≤ G, F ≢ G, then,
under mild conditions, the time constant for G is strictly smaller than that for
F." That is the shape of what we want — a *strict* inequality against a trivial
comparison — and it is exactly what the deterministic setting cannot borrow,
because it is proved by re-randomising the environment.
[Durrett, *Oriented Percolation in Two Dimensions*, Ann. Probab. 12 (1984)](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-probability/volume-12/issue-4/Oriented-Percolation-in-Two-Dimensions/10.1214/aop/1176993140.full),
fetched, landing page only: "This paper is a self-contained survey of most of the
results known about oriented percolation." **UNVERIFIED**: the specific statement
"the edge speed `α(p)` satisfies `α(p) < 1` for every `p < 1`" — searched
projecteuclid and arXiv for Durrett 1984 and for "edge speed oriented percolation
subadditive"; found only paywalled or landing-page text. The claim is standard and
a theorist should not need it, since §3.2 kills the transfer anyway.
Wolfram 1986 §5 is held locally (`sources/wolfram-1986-random-sequence-generation.txt`,
lines 594–615): "As a first approximation, one may assume that the motion of this
front depends only on the neighbouring values … If possible sets of site values
occured with equal probabilities, the front should thus follow a biased random
walk, advancing at average speed 1/4. In practice, however, Fig. 5.1 shows that
the front can retreat by many sites in a single time step." — his own note that
the walk model is wrong is the same observation this section quantifies.

**The test.** Run `G` on the settled background and see whether its speed is
above or below 1/2. Done: `explorer/rosetta_greedy2.mjs`, 2·10^8 rows across
diagonals 87,868 to 10^8 (channel-free: the next eventually-white diagonal is at
1,420,878,968), speed **0.50106**, with all twenty 10^7-step blocks between
0.50076 and 0.50133; `explorer/rosetta_greedy.mjs` gets 0.5044, 0.5044, 0.5045
from three different starts on the shorter channel-free stretch 780..53,206. The
excess above 1/2 survives three controls run through the same code path
(`explorer/rosetta_control.mjs`, 4·10^7 rows each, σ = 0.00008): pseudo-random
bits of density 1/2 give 0.49999; the *real* settled words with each diagonal's
phase randomised give 0.50001; random words with the real period profile give
0.49998. So the estimator is unbiased, the words themselves are unbiased, and the
entire excess is **the phase alignment between neighbouring settled words** — and
it pushes the front away from the wall. (An off-by-one in which cell `G` reads
would be a phase shift and could fake exactly this, so the convention is pinned
independently: it is the same one that reproduces the *real* front's speed-1 runs
cell for cell in §3.3, 53,251 of 53,251.)

The induction itself is the kind of claim that survives an off-by-one in the index
convention, so it was checked against the engine rather than trusted:
`explorer/rosetta_compare.mjs` runs `F` from the real rows and `G` from the
settled words in the same loop, started together at `F(1000) = G(1000) = −220`, and
finds **`G(t) > F(t)` on 0 of 129,000 rows**, ending with `F = −32,470` against
`G = −76,792`. Over those rows the real front runs at 0.25000 and `G` at 0.50438
until row 106,782 — where `G` reaches the white channel `κ = 53,208` and then
advances at speed 1 for the remaining 23,218 rows without a single stall, while
the real front had stepped over that channel 35,000 rows earlier and was at
`κ = 79,924`. One run, both halves of this document. To falsify: exhibit a
channel-free stretch on which `G`'s speed is below 1/2, or a row where `G > F`.

**What it would give.** A bound below 1 on `λ₋` for this pair — the thing
crystals A3 says is unavailable for arbitrary pairs — of the explicit form
`−F(t₁) ≤ −F(t₀) + 0.50106 (t₁ − t₀)` on every channel-free stretch, checkable by
a finite computation of the settled words and a two-line induction. The stretches
chain: `G ≤ F` forces `κ_G ≤ κ_F`, so `G` reaches each white channel *later* than
the front does (measured: `G` arrives at `κ = 53,208` on row 106,782, by which row
the front is already at `κ = 79,924`), and restarting `G` at that row from the
front's own position covers the next stretch. So the argument tiles all of time,
and what it needs to be an asymptotic theorem is a uniform bound on the greedy
speed over every stretch. What would remain after all that is everything that
matters: the wall needs 1/2, and this is 0.50106.

### 3.2 The environment contains infinite open channels, and the front must dodge them — which is a two-line theorem, not a coincidence

**The claim.** At every eventually-white diagonal the settled background contains
an infinite straight white channel, along which *any* front rides at speed 1 for
ever; the real front never enters one, and that is provable from
`leftDiagonal_periodicFrom_pow` alone; so at diagonals 3, 8, 29, 400, 53208,
58287 and 87867 the front's retreat is **forced by a theorem**, and this is the
only place on the board where the retreats are tied to the settled region at all.

The argument: suppose `κ(t) = κ` and `S_{κ−1}` is identically white. The cell left
of the front is settled (it lies left of the leftmost deviation), so it equals
`S_{κ−1}` at that index, so it is white, so the front advances, so `κ` is unchanged
— for ever. But then diagonal `κ` of the picture differs from `S_κ` at every index
past that point, contradicting the fact that diagonal `κ` is eventually periodic
with settled word `S_κ`. Hence `κ(t) ≠ κ` for every `t`.

**The dictionary.**

| Here | Percolation |
|---|---|
| eventually-white diagonal `S_m ≡ 0` | an infinite open oriented path in the environment |
| the channel `κ = m + 1` | the diagonal a front would ride for ever |
| the front's jump over it (e.g. 53,206 → 53,209 at row 71,116) | the edge is forced off the infinite open path |
| the period doubling at `m` (Rowland 2006 §6) | the channels are exactly the places where the environment's period changes |
| "the periods are unbounded" (this project's `leftDiagonal_period_unbounded`) | there are **infinitely many** infinite open paths |
| **Seam 1** | this is a probability-zero event under every Bernoulli or ergodic environment measure, so no percolation theorem covers it. The deterministic environment is *worse* than a random one for this purpose |
| **Seam 2** | the reason the real front avoids them is not a property of the environment at all — it is the settling theorem, i.e. the qualitative version of the very wall we want quantitatively. So the escape hatch is closed, but by an argument that is circular if you try to iterate it |

**What it leans on.** Nothing outside the project: `leftDiagonal_periodicFrom_pow`
(board), `rule30_left_local_law` (crystals A2), and Rowland 2006 §6 for the fact
that the settled words branch exactly at the eventually-white diagonals
(`sources/rowland-2006-local-nested-structure.txt`; the project already relies on
this in `explorer/forbit.mjs`).

**The test.** Track the real front and check whether `κ(t)` ever equals a channel.
Done: `explorer/rosetta_visits.mjs`, 130,000 rows, `κ` reaching 97,529, 53,252
diagonals visited: **none of the seven channels visited**, each skipped by an
explicit jump — 2→9 (row 2), 28→30 (row 31), 398→403 (row 501), 53,206→53,209
(row 71,116), 58,286→58,288 (row 77,910), 87,865→87,868 (row 117,324). And the
converse for the comparison walker, which does not have the settling theorem
working for it: `explorer/rosetta_greedy.mjs` started at row 18 escapes on diagonal
29 and never stalls again (speed 0.991 by row 3,000); started at row 500 it escapes
on diagonal 400; and `explorer/rosetta_compare.mjs`, started at row 1,000 on the
real front's own position, escapes on diagonal 53,208 at row 106,782 and then runs
at speed 1 for 23,218 rows. To falsify: find a `t` with
`κ(t) ∈ {3, 8, 29, 400, 53208, 58287, 87867}`.

**What it would give.** A small, self-contained Lean lemma (`the front never rides
a white channel`) which is the first statement on the board linking a retreat to
the settled region, and — more valuable — it closes off, for good, every argument
of the form "compare the front to a monotone process driven by the background".
Any such process escapes at diagonal 29. What remains: everything quantitative.

### 3.3 The run-length bound: `λ₋ ≤ 1 − 1/P`, and `P` is the *other* wall

**The claim.** Every speed-1 run of the front is exactly a white run of one
settled word read from the front's arrival phase; a periodic word of period `p`
that is not identically white has no white run of length `p`; therefore the
front's speed over any stretch is at most `1 − 1/P` where `P` is the largest
settled period it has met — so the front-speed wall and the period wall
`leftDiagonal_period_le` are the same wall seen from two sides, and a *logarithmic*
period bound (which the data supports and nobody has) would give a real speed
bound where the board's linear one gives nothing.

**The dictionary.**

| Here | Constrained coding / run-length-limited codes |
|---|---|
| the settled word `S_k` | a periodic codeword |
| its longest white run `W_k` | the `k`-constraint of a `(d,k)` RLL code |
| a speed-1 run of the front | a maximal all-white stretch traversed |
| a stall | the mandatory symbol that ends a run |
| the speed bound `M/(M+1)` | the rate ceiling of the constraint |
| `W_k ≤ p_k − 1` for non-white words | a run as long as the period forces the all-zero codeword |
| the eventually-white diagonals | the degenerate codeword with no constraint at all |
| **Seam 1** | the constraint is not uniform: `W_k` reaches 29 by diagonal 823,345 and the periods are unbounded, so `M` is not a constant and the ceiling drifts to 1 |
| **Seam 2** | the bound is loose by a factor of four. `M = 29` gives 0.967 where the truth is 0.2497 — this row is a *bound*, not a description |

**What it leans on.** The board: `leftDiagonal_periodicFrom_pow`, crystals A2, and
the computed period profile (NKS p. 871 via `explorer/orbit32.mjs`: the sixth
doubling at 2,107,985,255, so the settled periods are ≤ 32 below that). Nothing
external.

**The test.** Two, both run. `explorer/rosetta_visits.mjs`: over 53,251 speed-1
runs the achieved run equals the white run of `S_{κ−1}` from the arrival index in
**53,251 of 53,251** cases, and never exceeds the word's longest white run; the
longest achieved run is 15, on diagonal 89,537, whose neighbour word has period 32
and longest white run exactly 15. `explorer/rosetta_runs.mjs`, 2·10^6 diagonals
from the recurrence alone: the longest white run over all non-white settled words
is 29 (at diagonal 823,345, period 32), the largest ratio `W_k/p_k` is 15/16, the
mean `W_k` is 4.48, the mean black density is 0.5000, and the run histogram decays
by a factor near 2 per unit — the words are white-run-indistinguishable from fair
coins except for the seven all-white ones. To falsify: exhibit a `k` with
`W_k ≥ p_k` and `S_k` not identically white, or a front run longer than the
neighbour's longest white run.

**What it would give.** `λ₋ ≤ 31/32 = 0.969` for every row below about 2.8·10^9,
by finite computation — again a bound below 1 where A3 says none is available. And
a bridge: with a period bound `p_k ≤ C log k` the front bound becomes
`1 − 1/(C log t)`, whose deficit `t/(C log t)` is sublinear; with the board's
linear wall `p_k ≤ k + 1` it degenerates to `F(T) ≥ −T + 1`, i.e. nothing. So this
route is worth a theorist's time only alongside a logarithmic period bound.

### 3.4 Not KPZ: the front is diffusive, and the field that does fit predicts break points

**The claim.** The left damage front of rule 30 is not in the KPZ class. Its
wandering exponent is 1/2, which is the exponent of the *edge* of a supercritical
oriented percolation cluster (Kuczek's CLT), not the 1/3 of a KPZ interface; and
the mechanism behind that CLT — regeneration points — is precisely the structure
the project has already proved the *environment* does not have, which says to look
for it in the band instead.

**The dictionary.**

| Here | KPZ / edge CLT |
|---|---|
| `F(t)` | the height at a point, or the edge `r_t` |
| `D(t) = F(t) + λ t` | the fluctuation about the drift |
| measured `σ(s) ~ s^0.50` over scales 8–2048 | KPZ predicts `s^{1/3}`; the OP edge CLT predicts `s^{1/2}` |
| the retreat-size law (1:87259, 2:8941, 3:4140, 4:3461, 5:624, …, 13:1 over 4·10^5 rows) | the jump law of the edge process; note it is **not** geometric — 3 and 4 are nearly equal |
| Kuczek's break points | rows at which the front's future is independent of its past |
| **Seam 1** | the exponent at the largest measured scales (4,096–16,384) rises to 0.58, and the residual drift per step there is 5·10⁻⁴, so the front's *local* drift itself wanders on long time scales: the measured 0.2497 is not a settled constant, it is a window average (0.2200 at row 1,000; 0.2512 at 10^4; 0.2526 at 10^5; 0.2460 at 4·10^5) |
| **Seam 2** | there is no free energy, no temperature, no noise, and nothing to take an expectation over — every "exponent" here is a slope fitted to one deterministic sequence |
| **Seam 3** | KPZ describes a *d*-dimensional interface; the damage cluster in a 1D automaton has only two boundary points, one of which is deterministic. The vantage's KPZ half was structurally the wrong half before the measurement said so |

**What it leans on.**
[Kuczek, *The Central Limit Theorem for the Right Edge of Supercritical Oriented
Percolation*, Ann. Probab. 17 (1989)](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-probability/volume-17/issue-4/The-Central-Limit-Theorem-for-the-Right-Edge-of-Supercritical/10.1214/aop/1176991157.full),
fetched: "This article contains a proof of the conjectured central limit theorem
for the growth of the right-hand edge for supercritical oriented percolation. The
technique of proof, finding points with regeneration-type properties termed
'break points,' may also apply to other processes."
[Bagnoli, Boccara, Rechtman, *On damage spreading transitions*, arXiv:cond-mat/9508106](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9508106),
fetched: "we are able to show analitically that the evolution of the damage
between two systems driven by the same noise has the same structure of a directed
percolation problem." Their setting is a *stochastic* automaton with a noise
parameter; ours has none, which is why §4 records the DP class as dead here.

**The test.** `explorer/rosetta_kpz.mjs`, 4·10^5 rows: fit the drift over the
second half, then measure the variance of increments of `D` at dyadic scales. Local
slopes 0.509, 0.506, 0.497, 0.498, 0.491, 0.508, 0.512, 0.524 for `s = 16 … 2048`,
then 0.577, 0.606, 0.583. Not 1/3 anywhere, at any scale. To falsify: find a scale
window where the exponent is 1/3, or show the 0.58 at large scales is the true
asymptotic rather than drift wander.

**What it would give.** It removes KPZ from the board and points at the one
transferable idea in the neighbourhood: a *break point* — a row at which the band's
future does not depend on its past. `explorer/rosetta_band.mjs` measures the
obvious candidate: the front's leftmost solid block of deviations has length 1 on
107,792 of 200,000 rows, so that event is far too common to be a regeneration on
its own. What remains is to find the right conditioning.

### 3.5 The retreats are the death of the band's leftmost block, and the wall is an inequality between two measured rates

**The claim.** The front's motion decomposes exactly as
`λ₋ = (white density the front sees) − (mean retreat per row)`, measured over
159,999 rows in `explorer/maskfront.mjs` as
`0.2497 = 0.5904 − 54,509/159,999`; so, granted that the limits exist, the wall
`leftDiagonal_onset_le` says exactly "mean retreat per row ≥ (white density seen)
− 1/2", i.e. `0.3407 ≥ 0.0904` — an inequality between one quantity the background
supplies and one only the band does, with a factor of 3.8 of measured slack. It is
a rearrangement rather than a reduction: both sides are averages along the front's
own trajectory, so neither is a statistic of the environment. Its value is that it
puts the whole difficulty in one term and says which one.

**The dictionary.**

| Here | Interacting particle systems / defect gas |
|---|---|
| a cell of the band `E` | a defect (particle) in the regular domain |
| the settled region `S` | the regular domain the defects live in |
| the leftmost solid block of the band, length `b` | the leading defect cluster |
| a retreat by `r` | the leading cluster annihilates and the edge falls to the next |
| the four kill types of `explorer/maskfront.mjs` (masked 4%, two transients meeting in one `\|\|` 68%, …) | the reaction table of the particle species |
| `P(advance \| b) = 0.58–0.61` for every `b` from 1 to 10 | the advance is a function of the background alone — the local law, confirmed from the inside |
| **Seam 1** | the retreat size is *not* a function of `b`: `r = b` on 75.3% of retreats, but that is carried by `b = 1` (where `r = 1` always); for `b ≥ 2` it holds only 21.5% of the time, and `b = 2` produces retreats up to 11 |
| **Seam 2** | particle systems have a conserved or Markovian particle count; here the band's density near the front is 0.509, essentially the background's own 1/2, and nothing is conserved |
| **Seam 3** | the retreats are the only part of the whole problem with no representation in the settled region, so this row is where the dictionary ends rather than where it breaks |

**What it leans on.** **UNVERIFIED**: Hanson & Crutchfield, *Computational
mechanics of cellular automata: an example* (Physica D 103, 1997), which builds a
"domain filter" separating a regular domain from its defects and derives defect
equations of motion for elementary rules 18 and 54 — the closest published
technique to reading our band as a defect gas. Searched: web search on
"Hanson Crutchfield computational mechanics cellular automata regular domains
defects filter"; the ScienceDirect page returns HTTP 403, the UC Davis PDF
(`csc.ucdavis.edu/~cmg/papers/ECA54.pdf`) fetched as unreadable binary, and the
UC Davis abstract page 404s. The search summary asserts rule 18's "dislocation
gas agree[s] extremely well with the classical model of annihilating diffusive
particles", but I could not fetch a sentence, so treat every word of this row as
a lead, not a citation. It is also the reason §6 names that field.

**The test.** `explorer/rosetta_band.mjs`, 200,000 rows, window 64 cells right of
the front. The front's own view of the background is white on 0.58942 of rows,
against 0.50106 for the never-retreating walker on the same background. The front
sits far to the right of the walker and yet reads a *whiter* neighbourhood, by
0.088 — the strongest single sign that the front is an extremal object selected by
survival rather than a walker following a rule. (A correlation, not a mechanism: I
did not establish that the retreat causes the whiter read.) To
falsify the decomposition: check `0.5904 − 0.3407 = 0.2497` against
`explorer/maskfront.mjs` at a different depth.

**What it would give.** A restatement of the wall with the background eliminated:
prove that the mean retreat per row exceeds the excess of the front's white
density over 1/2. That is one inequality between two rates, both measurable, and
it is the honest target this vantage produces.

## 4. Died in translation

- **Kingman subadditivity / the existence of `λ₋`.** The front from row `t` is the
  front of the pair `(evolve t, S at t)`, so the natural functional is a cocycle,
  not a subadditive one, and the only uniform bound over pairs is speed 1. Crystals
  A3 *is* this obstruction, stated in the project's own vocabulary before I got
  here. Worth saying plainly: **nobody has proved that `F(t)/t` converges at all** —
  every number in this document, including 0.2497, is a window average, and the
  window averages move (0.2200, 0.2512, 0.2526, 0.2460 at rows 10^3, 10^4, 10^5,
  4·10^5).
- **The KPZ class.** Measured wandering exponent 0.50 over scales 8–2048, rising to
  0.58 at the top three scales; 1/3 appears nowhere. Seam: the damage cluster of a
  1D automaton has two boundary points, not an interface.
- **The Eden model / shape theorem.** The damage cluster's shape is a cone whose
  right side is exactly speed 1 by left-permutivity and whose left side *is* the
  question. There is no shape to prove a theorem about — the "limit shape" has one
  unknown parameter and that parameter is `λ₋`.
- **Directed percolation universality for damage spreading (Grassberger's
  conjecture, Bagnoli et al.).** The DP class describes a *transition* tuned by a
  noise parameter, with a nonzero healing probability. Rule 30 has no parameter, no
  noise, and its damage never dies out. Nothing about critical exponents transfers,
  and there is no critical point to sit at.
- **Interface depinning.** The depinning transition is the boundary between `v = 0`
  and `v > 0`; ours is between `v < 1` and `v = 1`, so the pinned phase is
  everything and the order parameter carries no information.
- **Max-plus / tropical cycle mean, and ergodic optimisation.** If the environment's
  driving orbit — the sequence of adjacent pairs `(S_{k−1}, S_k)` — ever repeated,
  the front's speed would be a maximum cycle mean of a finite graph, computable
  exactly by Karp's algorithm, and the wall would be a finite check. It never
  repeats: the pair map has in-degree one and the project's own C1/C2 (attack
  document of 2026-09-07) show no two adjacent diagonals ever eventually agree. The
  graph is a ray. This kills the whole "compute the speed exactly" family.
- **Renewal / regeneration.** Same seam from the other side: no repeated pair means
  no renewal kernel. Kuczek's break points are the shape of what would work, and
  the obvious candidate (the front's leftmost block having length 1) occurs on 54%
  of rows, far too often to be a regeneration.
- **Circle rotations and Sturmian words.** Riding one periodic word is a rotation
  and its speed would be a rotation number; the front changes word at every stall,
  so there is no single circle map. Died in one line.
- **Greedy lattice animals and paths (Cox–Gandolfi–Griffin–Kesten).** The law of
  large numbers there is for i.i.d. or ergodic masses and gives the *maximum* over
  paths; we have one deterministic array and need a bound on *one* path. Fetched
  ([arXiv:2409.14773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14773)): "In the case where the
  masses are i.i.d. and satisfy a certain moment condition, it is known that there
  exists a deterministic A ≥ 0 such that the maximal mass A_n of an animal
  containing 0 with cardinal n satisfies A_n/n → A when n → ∞, almost surely."
  The seam is the phrase "i.i.d." and the phrase "almost surely", and both are
  fatal here. The *idea* survives as §3.1's walker `G`, which is the greedy path
  made deterministic — and it is worth recording that the greedy path is twice as
  fast as the front (0.501 against 0.2497), so the optimisation framing overshoots
  by a factor of two.
- **"White density is 1/2, so the speed is 1/2".** The most natural heuristic in
  this whole vantage, and it is wrong in the direction that matters: measured
  0.50106, not 0.5, over 2·10^8 rows, with 13.4σ significance and every 10^7-block
  above 1/2. The controls locate the excess exactly: real words with randomised
  phases give 0.50001, random words with the real periods give 0.49998, so the
  excess is inter-diagonal phase alignment, and it points the wrong way.
- **Hierarchical / 2-adic percolation lattices.** The environment's periods are
  powers of two and the doublings are at diagonals 3, 8, 29, 400, 87867,
  2,107,985,255 — gaps 5, 21, 371, 87,467, 2.0·10^9, each roughly the square of the
  one before. Any
  renormalisation argument would need to see two levels at once, and no measurable
  window contains two.
- **Resonance capture (KAM).** The eventually-white diagonals look exactly like
  resonances a trajectory must avoid being captured by. There is no Hamiltonian, no
  small divisor, no measure; the only thing that transferred was the *question*,
  and §3.2 answers it with a two-line CA argument instead.
- **My own first framing, recorded because it cost an hour.** I began by trying to
  bound the front by the *density* of black cells in the settled words, reasoning
  that a positive density of blockers gives a speed below 1. It does — that is
  §3.3 — but the density is the wrong statistic: it is exactly 0.5000 and gives
  exactly the critical value. The statistic that carries the bound is the *longest
  white run*, and the statistic that carries the truth is neither, it is the
  retreat rate.

## 5. What to hand the theorist

**Topic 1 — the comparison walker, and the 0.001 that separates it from the wall.**
The claim to falsify: *`G(t) ≤ F(t)` for all `t ≥ t₀` whenever `G(t₀) = F(t₀)`,
where `G` is the never-retreating front driven by the settled words alone.* It is a
two-case induction on `rule30_left_local_law` (crystals A2) plus "the cell left of
the leftmost deviation is settled", and it is small enough to state in Lean against
`settledConfig`; checked against the engine at 0 violations over 129,000 rows
(`explorer/rosetta_compare.mjs`). The dictionary row it depends on is the one
mapping *stall* to *wait* rather than to *die*. What it settles: `λ₋ ≤ speed(G)`,
and `speed(G)` is a finite computation on the settled words — 0.50106 over 2·10^8
rows across diagonals 87,868 to 10^8, 0.5044 over the shorter stretch. That is the
first bound below 1 on a left damage front in this project, and crystals A3 does
not forbid it, because A3 is about arbitrary pairs and this is about one.

**And the same induction is the reason not to spend another session on any
argument that drops the retreats.** `G` is not one comparison among many: it is
optimal, so 0.50106 is the exact ceiling of what the local law and the background
can prove together, and the wall needs 0.5. The depth that would settle whether the
gap is real rather than a slowly decaying transient: run
`explorer/rosetta_greedy2.mjs` to 10^10 diagonals and see whether the block speeds
stay above 1/2 (they are 0.50076–0.50133 over twenty 10^7-step blocks so far, none
below, and the excess is 13.4σ against the script's own random-background control,
which returns 0.49999).

**Topic 2 — the front never rides a white channel.** The claim to prove:
*for every `t`, the settled word `S_{κ(t)−1}` is not identically white*, where
`κ(t) = t + F(t)`. Two lines, from `leftDiagonal_periodicFrom_pow` and A2: if it
were, the front would advance for ever, and diagonal `κ(t)` would then disagree
with its own settled word at every later index. The dictionary row it depends on is
"eventually-white diagonal = infinite open oriented path". What it gives: the
retreats at rows 2 (which clears the channels at `κ = 3` and `κ = 8` in one jump),
31, 501, 71,116, 77,910 and 117,324 become forced rather than observed, and every
monotone-comparison route is closed globally rather than by measurement. Verified to depth 130,000 rows and `κ = 97,529` in
`explorer/rosetta_visits.mjs`, with the skipping jumps listed. This is the smaller
of the two and the more likely to close.

**If a captain wants an obstruction out of this document, it is the corollary of
Topic 1, and I am not the one who may write it there.** Stated in the project's
vocabulary: *`leftDiagonal_onset_le` is not a consequence of
`rule30_left_local_law` together with the settled region.* Any proof built from
those two ingredients bounds the front by the optimal background-driven trajectory
`G`, whose speed is 0.50106 on the 2·10^8 rows measured — above the 1/2 the wall
needs, with every 10^7-block above it and a random-background control at 0.49999.
The caveat that keeps this a sighting rather than a theorem is that 0.50106 is a
window average of a deterministic walk, and nobody has proved it does not fall
below 1/2 later; the honest form is "on every stretch measured so far". A proof of
the wall must reach into the transient band, and §3.5 says exactly which number in
the band it must reach for.

Not handed over, and deliberately: §3.3's run-length bound, because on its own it
proves 0.969 and its useful form needs a logarithmic bound on the settled periods,
which is a harder open problem than the one it would serve.

## 6. Next vantage

**Computational mechanics of cellular automata: regular domains, domain filters,
and defect (particle) dynamics — Crutchfield and Hanson's programme, applied to the
transient band rather than to the settled region.** Every route in this document
converges on one quantity that has no representation in the settled background at
all: the retreat rate, 0.3407 cells per row, which is exactly the difference
between what the environment can prove (0.501) and what is true (0.2497). The band
is a gas of defects in a regular domain — the project has already identified the
domain (crystal 47, the settled picture) and even the reaction table (the four kill
types of `explorer/maskfront.mjs`: two transients meeting in a single `||` at 68%,
a black settled neighbour at 4%), which is precisely the data a computational-
mechanics analysis produces as its *output*. That literature builds a filter that
subtracts the domain and leaves the particles, then derives equations of motion for
them; for rules 18 and 54 the filtered defects reportedly behave like annihilating
diffusive particles. Nobody on this board has read it, and I could not fetch a
readable copy from where I stood (403 from the publisher, a binary PDF from the
author's site, a 404 on the abstract page), so its rows are the one part of this
sighting I am handing on as an address rather than as a dictionary. The specific
question to take there: **is the left front of the band the leftmost particle of an
annihilating system whose death rate can be bounded below by the domain's own
statistics?** If yes, the missing 0.09 — the gap between the front's white density
0.5894 and the 1/2 the wall needs — is exactly a death rate, and death rates are
the one thing that literature computes.

A second vantage I could not reach and would rank next: the settled words as an
**automatic sequence** (Christol, Cobham, algebraic power series over `F₂`). If the
settled picture is 2-automatic — its power-of-two periods and Rowland's recurrence
both suggest it — then the phase alignment that produces the 0.00106 excess in
§3.1 is a computable quantity rather than a measured one, and the greedy walker's
speed becomes an algebraic object instead of a simulation. I did not test
automaticity and it is cheap to test: the settled centre column `settledCenter` and
the word sequence `S_k` are both generated by `explorer/settledwords.mjs`, and a
2-kernel check is a few lines.
