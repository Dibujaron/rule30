# Attack: the masking mechanism in the transient band, why a diagonal settles before its drivers

Topic: `centerColumn_other_isEventuallyPeriodic_of_center`. Theorist: Sextant, 2026-09-08. Everything below is an argument, not a proof; every number names the script and the depth it came from. Scripts: `explorer/maskfront.mjs` (the front and the onsets, 160,000 rows, onsets final for diagonals to 110,000) and `explorer/maskabsorb.mjs` (how a settled diagonal absorbs a transient driver, same depth).

## 1. The residual, in one paragraph

Time runs down the picture. Every left diagonal settles into a repeating word from some onset on, and the settled cells form a region on the left whose boundary, the seam, runs down the picture about a quarter of the way in from the centre. Between the seam and the centre lies the transient band, and the centre column is the band's right edge: every centre cell is index zero of its diagonal, before the onset. The wall asks whether a repeating centre column would force another column to repeat. This topic is not that residual but the gap beneath it that the last attack left open: the onsets of the diagonals are measured at a third of the diagonal number, while the bound the board's two reset lemmas give when iterated is twice the diagonal number, because a diagonal often settles at an index where its two drivers are still transient. The question was what mechanism lets it, and the guess on record was the `||` of the rule: a black cell of the diagonal hides the driver's transient behind it. What would have to be true for this to bear on the wall is that the mechanism, made exact, says something about the band's right edge, the centre, and not only its left edge, the seam. It does not: the mechanism is the motion of a damage front, its exact form is a theorem already on the board, and everything it determines lives on the seam. Section 4 says what that leaves.

## 2. Why the known routes fail

- **The diagonal structure never reaches the centre column** (obstructions, first entry): every diagonal theorem is about the settled part; the centre is transient on every diagonal. This attack is about the band's *left* edge, and it confirms the entry rather than escaping it.
- **The settled part does not depend on the centre column** and **the left of the picture knows one integer** (fourth and fifth entries): the seam is a damage front and the settled region behind it is the seed's. This attack makes the seam exact, cell for cell, and it stays a fact about the left.
- **Iterating the reset lemmas builds a front that cannot retreat** (new, appended today as the sixth entry): the reset lemma is exact on the diagonals the front visits, and the diagonals that settle before their drivers are exactly the ones it skips, at retreats of the front that no induction on the diagonal index can express. The onset wall is equivalent to a speed bound below `1/2` on this one front, which is a statement of the kind crystals A3 says is not available.
- **Considered and not pursued:** a proof of the front bound from the structure of the settled words (power-of-two periods, the recurrence). The front advances only across white cells of settled words and a diagonal with a long white run is exactly an eventually-white one, of which there are seven below 200,000; but the front sits on a diagonal only at that diagonal's transient cells, and nothing bounds how long it rides. I have no argument, only the measured `0.25`.

## 3. Candidate claims

Notation, used throughout: `D_k(j) = leftDiagonal k j`; `S_k` the settled word of diagonal `k`, which on the board is `D_k(j + 2^k)` for `j ≥ 0` (past the onset by `leftDiagonal_periodicFrom_pow`, and a multiple of the period); a cell is *transient* when `T k j : D_k(j) ≠ D_k(j + 2^k)`; the onset `N_k` is one more than the last transient index; in row coordinates the cell `(t, x)` is `D_{t+x}(-x)`. The *front* is `F(t) = min { x ∈ [-t, 0] : T (t + x) (-x) }`, which exists for every `t ≥ 18` (the first transient cell is `(18, 0)`: `s(k) = c(k)` for `k ≤ 17`), and `κ(t) = t + F(t)` is the diagonal the front sits on. Every transient cell at time `t` lies at `x ≥ F(t)`.

### C1. The two masking laws, in diagonal coordinates

**The claim.** In English: a transient does not pass straight down a diagonal through a black driver, and does not pass down into a settled diagonal through that diagonal's own black cell; in both cases the transient is stopped exactly when the cell doing the stopping is black. Precisely, for all `k, j`:

- (a) if `T (k+2) j`, `¬T (k+1) (j+1)` and `¬T k (j+2)`, then `T (k+2) (j+1) ↔ D_{k+1}(j+1) = false`;
- (b) if `¬T (k+2) j`, `T (k+1) (j+1)` and `¬T k (j+2)`, then `T (k+2) (j+1) ↔ D_{k+2}(j) = false`.

(a) is the front's law: the cell below-left of the front is transient iff the settled cell beside the front is white. (b) is the notebook's masking: a settled diagonal stays settled through a transient driver iff its own previous cell is black.

**What it would give.** The vocabulary for C2 and C3: the band `E = picture xor S` is a damage pattern between two rule 30 evolutions, and these are its local law without mentioning the settled configuration or `column_settledConfig_eq`, which is still open. Nothing about the centre.

**Falsification.** `explorer/maskfront.mjs`, 160,000 rows. (a) at every step of the front: 159,999 steps, 0 failures of `F(t+1) ≥ F(t) - 1` with equality iff the cell `(t, F(t) - 1)` is white; 319 full re-scans of the row left of the front found nothing left of `F(t) - 1`. (b) is checked in `explorer/maskabsorb.mjs` as the case "own black cell masks" (its classification is asserted to predict agreement at every cell it counts, and the assertion never fired). **Distrust:** both are two-line consequences of the rule and cannot fail; what the engine is really checking is that my settled words are right, that is, that `S_k = F(S_{k-2}, S_{k-1})` with the seed's seven branch bits gives the picture's own settled words to diagonal 120,044 (a wrong branch bit at 53,208 would have produced an avalanche of front violations past row 71,000; there were none). The survival is a check of the settled-word orbit, not of the laws.

**Novelty.** New phrasing of a theorem on the board: `rule30_left_local_law` (crystals A2) read on the pair (seed's row `t`, `S`'s row `t`) in diagonal coordinates, with the two cases the general law does not separate. In print: Wolfram 1986 §5, lines 594–610 of the extraction, states (a) in words for the difference pattern of two random rows ("when `a_{-1} = 0` the front advances by one site, independent of the values of `a_1`; when `a_{-1} = 1` the front remains stationary … or retreats"). Searched rowland (both), kopra, jen, kurka for transient, onset, front, speed, propagat, damage, regular, boundary: Kopra's "spreading speed" (Definition 3.3) is the speed of the leftmost black cell of a number-like configuration, `1` for rule 30, not a damage front; Rowland has no transient statement; Jen's speeds are the cone edges.

**Route.** Done, in the kernel: `explorer/scratch_masking.lean`, accepted by `lake env lean` on 2026-09-08 with no output (`#print axioms`: `propext, Quot.sound` for the two `M`-forms, plus `Classical.choice` for the `2^k` form), imports only `LeftDiagonalRecurrence`, `LeftDiagonalPeriodicFromPow` and `PeriodicFromMul`. It states (a) and (b) for an arbitrary shift `M` (hypotheses: the two settled cells agree with themselves `M` later, the transient one does not), each proved by `leftDiagonal_recurrence` at `i = j` and `i = j + M`, four `generalize`s and a sixteen-case `decide`; and (a) in the `2^k` form above, with `leftDiagonal_periodicFrom_pow` and `periodicFrom_mul` moving the two drivers to the common shift `2^(k+2)`. Two or three S nodes under `leftDiagonal_onset_le`; the hard step was nowhere.

### C2. The onset of a diagonal the front visits is the reset lemma's value exactly

**The claim.** In English: the front never moves to an earlier diagonal; it rides a diagonal at speed 1 along a white run of the settled word beside it and leaves it for good at that word's next black cell; so the onset of every diagonal the front visits is the first black cell of the neighbouring settled word past the arrival index, and such a diagonal settles no earlier than its neighbour and at most one index before the diagonal two out. Precisely: `κ` is nondecreasing; if `κ(t) = k` and `κ(t-1) ≠ k`, with `j₀ = -F(t)`, then `N_k = min { i > j₀ : S_{k-1}(i) = true }`, `N_{k-1} ≤ j₀ + 1 ≤ N_k`, and `N_{k-2} ≤ j₀ + 2`.

**What it would give.** The exact content of `leftDiagonal_periodicFrom_step_of_black` on the seed: on the front the reset lemma has no slack. And the negative half that answers the topic: a diagonal that settles strictly before its neighbour is never on the front, so "settling before the drivers" is not a mechanism at the seam but a property of the diagonals the front skips, which it skips by retreating.

**Falsification.** `explorer/maskfront.mjs`: `κ` never decreased over 160,000 rows; of the diagonals `18..110,000`, the front visited 60,065 and skipped 49,917 (one of the skipped diagonals in that range has no transient cell at all); for all 60,065 visited diagonals the onset equals the departure index plus one and equals the first black of `S_{k-1}` past the arrival (0 failures each); no visited diagonal settles before its neighbour; 4,507 settle before the diagonal two out, every one by exactly one index, as the claim allows. Of the skipped diagonals 47,343 settle strictly before their neighbour and 22,706 before the diagonal two out (20,133 before both). **Distrust:** the identity is forced by C1(a) once "everything left of the front is settled" holds, which is the definition of the front; so the check is again a check that `F` is the true leftmost deviation (the full re-scans) and that the settled words are right. The visited/skipped split is a fact about which cells are leftmost, not about periodicity.

**Novelty.** Not in print. Rowland 2006 has the reset (his Lemma 3, the "bijectivity condition") as a criterion for the eventual period, not for the onset; Wolfram 1986 §6 lines 802–810 says the regular/irregular boundary moves at `0.25` "in analogy" with the damage front and gives no identification. Nearest on the board: crystal 45 (the onset dichotomy), which is this claim without the front, and therefore with the slack that becomes the `2.00 k` of the reset front.

**Route.** From C1(a) by induction on the run, `evolve_left_diagonals_isEventuallyPeriodic` to end it, and the observation that after a stop the front can never catch the diagonal again (`F(t') ≥ F(t) - (t' - t) + 1`). Needs a definition of `F` on the board (a `Nat.find` over the transient predicate at time `t`, which is decidable and bounded). Size M with C1; no hard step, much index bookkeeping.

### C3. The onset wall is a speed bound on the front

**The claim.** In English: every left diagonal has settled by index `k` if and only if the seam never runs faster than half a cell per row. Precisely: `(∀ k, ∃ p > 0, ∃ N ≤ k, PeriodicFrom (leftDiagonal k) p N) ↔ (∀ t ≥ 18, 2 · F(t) + t ≥ 1)`. In `ℕ` and without `∃`, the left side is `∀ k j, k ≤ j → leftDiagonal k j = leftDiagonal k (j + 2^k)`.

**What it would give.** It says what `leftDiagonal_onset_le` is: a bound on the speed of one damage front, between the seed and the settled row, and nothing else. A prover attacking the wall by induction on `k` is attacking a monotone front (the sixth obstruction); a proof has to bound a speed.

**Falsification.** `explorer/maskfront.mjs`: over `t = 18..159,999`, `min (2F(t) + t) = 17` at `t = 19`, and the quantity grows like `0.5 t`; the largest `-F(t)/t` for `t ≥ 1000` is `0.2568` at `t = 38,460`; the largest onset ratio `N_k / k` for `k ≥ 1000` is `0.3455` at `k = 28,584`, and `0.2568 / (1 - 0.2568) = 0.3455`: the same event seen along a diagonal (`38,460 - 9,877 = 28,583` is the diagonal the front sat on). Net speed `0.2497` over 160,000 rows, `F(t)/t` at `t = 10^4, 5·10^4, 10^5, 1.5·10^5`: `0.2512, 0.2514, 0.2526, 0.2495`. **Distrust:** the equivalence is a coordinate change and cannot fail; the numbers are a margin of `0.25` against `0.5`, measured, and the front's worst windows so far are `0.257`; nothing says a window of `0.5` never occurs, and speed `1` is the worst case for an arbitrary pair (crystals A3). The measurement is of the seed's own front only.

**Novelty.** The wall's docstring already attributes it to Wolfram's quarter; the equivalence, and the front as the difference from a specific second evolution rather than a "region of regularity", are not stated anywhere I hold. Searched as for C1.

**Route.** Both directions are index arithmetic on the definitions: a transient cell `(t, x)` has `x ≥ F(t)`, index `-x` and diagonal `t + x`, so `2F(t) + t ≥ 1` gives index `≤ (t - 1)/2 ≤ k - 1`; conversely the front cell of diagonal `κ(t)` has index `-F(t) ≤ N_κ - 1 ≤ κ - 1`. Size M once `F` is defined. No route to either side.

### C4. What kills a transient, and why a diagonal settles before its drivers

**The claim.** In English: the diagonals that settle before their drivers are the ones the front skips; their last transient is killed inside the band, and the killer is mostly not a black settled cell but a second transient meeting the first in one `||`. The provable half: if `N_k < N_{k-1}` then the front never visits `k` (contrapositive of C2). The measured half has no statement; it is the answer to the topic's question, and it is a statistic.

**What it would give.** The answer to "why a diagonal settles before its drivers": because the band is a damage pattern whose interior is dense (about half the cells of the band deviate) and a rule 30 damage pattern annihilates itself wherever two deviations meet in an `||` that is `true` in both pictures, or in an `xor` with equal inputs. The notebook's mechanism, a black settled cell masking a transient driver, is real and is the only mechanism on the front, and it is a minority everywhere else.

**Falsification.** `explorer/maskfront.mjs`, the last transient of each diagonal to 110,000, classified by the three cells its successor on the diagonal reads (`L`, `C` = the neighbour on diagonal `k - 1`, `R` = the transient itself): visited diagonals: all 60,065 killed by a black settled neighbour (`C` settled and black), as C2 says. Skipped diagonals (49,917): neighbour transient too, one black and one white (`C ≠ R`, the `||` is `true` in both pictures): 33,704; all three deviating with `C = R`: 7,273; the left cell transient and the neighbour white (cancellation in the `xor`): 6,827; masked by a black settled neighbour: 2,113, that is 4 %. Restricted to the 47,343 skipped diagonals that settle before their neighbour: 33,704 / 7,273 / 4,254 / 2,112. `explorer/maskabsorb.mjs`, the complementary count, every cell where a settled diagonal (past its final onset) sits directly under a transient driver, classified by the diagonal's own previous cell `R` (A: `R` settled and black, the notebook's masking; B: `R` transient, `C ≠ R`; C: `R` settled and white, `L` transient; D: all three transient, `C = R`): over the whole band, 803,061,382 cells whose cell below agrees, the four cases are each exactly a quarter (`0.250, 0.250, 0.250, 0.250`), the split of a fair coin on three cells; restricted to the 104,113 cells where the diagonal below is past its final onset, on 47,344 diagonals below 110,000 (the diagonals that settle before their neighbour, to within one), the split is A 54,729 (`0.526`), B 33,704 (`0.324`), C 8,407 (`0.081`), D 7,273 (`0.070`). So the two questions have different answers and I had conflated them: what *ends* a skipped diagonal's transient is mostly two transients in one `||` (B and D there are the same events as the kills counted above, 33,704 and 7,273 exactly), while what keeps the diagonal settled through the rest of its driver's transient, about 2.2 absorptions per such diagonal, is mostly its own black cell, the mechanism the notebook proposed. Pass 2 took 266 s; the script's agreement assertion fired once in a first run, at row 46, on a centre cell whose right neighbour is column 1, outside the band, which it now skips. **Distrust:** the classification is exhaustive (four cases of the rule) and asserted at every counted cell; the percentages are properties of the seed's band to this depth and the only thing they rule out is a single dominant mechanism. The band edge at `x = 0` is excluded from the absorption count (its right neighbour is column 1, outside the band), so the centre column's own absorptions are not in these numbers.

**Novelty.** Wolfram 1986 §5 line 604–607 notes that the front "can retreat by many sites in a single time step … when the rule yields the same image for multiple site value sequences", which is the same annihilation in words. The per-diagonal statistics are new and are not a claim.

**Route.** The provable half is C2's contrapositive. No route to anything else; there is nothing else to prove.

## 4. What survived

C1, C2 and C3 survived as exact statements, and they are of one kind: the seam of the transient band is a damage front between the seed and the settled row, its motion is `rule30_left_local_law`, the diagonals it visits obey the reset lemma with no slack, and the onset wall is the statement that this front's speed stays below a half. C4's provable half survived and its measured half is the answer to the topic: a diagonal settles before its drivers when the front retreats past it, and the transient that ends it is usually annihilated by another transient, not masked by a settled black cell; the notebook's mechanism is 4 % of the cases. None of this touches the wall this topic is filed under: every statement here is about the band's left edge, and `centerColumn_other_isEventuallyPeriodic_of_center` lives on its right edge, where there is no front. If a captain seeds anything, seed C1 first, two S nodes under `leftDiagonal_onset_le` with nothing but `leftDiagonal_recurrence` and periodicity behind them, then the definition of `F` and C3 as the wall's honest form; C2 is worth a node only if someone intends to attack the speed, and I know no one who can. ## 5. Claims that died

- **"A diagonal settles before its drivers because its own black cell masks the driver's transient"** (my notebook, 2026-09-07, the topic's premise). Died at the first count: of 49,917 skipped diagonals below 110,000, the last transient is masked by a black settled neighbour in 2,113 cases; 33,704 are two transients saturating one `||`. Witness: the first skipped diagonal past 1,000, wherever the reader looks; the counts are in `explorer/maskfront.mjs`.
- **"The front visits about two thirds of the diagonals"** (my estimate from a fair-coin neighbour): 0.546 to diagonal 110,000, because the neighbour is white on 59 % of the front's steps, not 50 %, and the front retreats on 26 %.
- **"The front retreats by at most one cell"** (Wolfram's simple walk, and what an isolated transient at the front would give): retreats of 2..11 cells occur, 6,919 of 41,799 retreats, the largest 11 at row 6,536; a block of transients at the front retreats by up to its length plus one.
- **"The visited diagonals settle no earlier than both drivers"**: false by one index for the diagonal two out, 4,507 times, exactly as the arithmetic allows (`N_{k-2} ≤ j₀ + 2`, `N_k ≥ j₀ + 1`).
- **"The front rides an eventually-white diagonal forever"** (a worry, not a claim): impossible, because the front cell is a transient of the *next* diagonal and that diagonal is eventually periodic; the longest ride to 160,000 rows is 15 cells, on diagonal 89,537 beside a word of period 32.
