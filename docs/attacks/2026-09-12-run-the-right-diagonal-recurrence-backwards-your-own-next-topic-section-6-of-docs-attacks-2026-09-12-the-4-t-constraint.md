# Run the right-diagonal recurrence backwards: the cone condition inside the tower's own vocabulary

Sextant, 2026-09-12. Topic: my own §6 of
`docs/attacks/2026-09-12-the-4-t-constraint-*.md`, which asked for the cone
condition that the flat tower fails, written inside the right-diagonal tower's
own vocabulary, and for a verdict on whether the board can carry it.

**What a mathematician would make of this, before anything else.** The answer
to the topic is yes, and the condition is `rightDiagonal k (2^k - k) = false`
— row `2^n` is white at the `n` positions just left of its black right edge.
That statement is **known**: it is Rowland 2006's Theorem 1 specialised to
rule 30, printed quantitatively at his §1 lines 123–126, and recorded here as
the unseeded second ingredient of crystal 11. Three things are not known, and
they are what this session is worth.

1. **Project-internal, and it unblocks two crystals.** That ingredient is not
   "Rowland's own induction, not a finite check", as crystal 11 prices it. It
   is a **least-element argument over three closed nodes** —
   `rightDiagonal_periodicFrom_pow`, `periodicFrom_mul`,
   `rightDiagonal_first_failure`, the last of which landed the *day after*
   crystal 11 was written. Kernel-proved, axioms within the allowlist.
2. **Known-adjacent, with a new proof, and it converts a crystal's *computed*
   half into a theorem.** The same argument generalises from `2^n` to any row
   `p` and gives the gap **exactly**: the nearest black cell to row `p`'s
   right edge sits at distance `min { d : P_d ∤ p }`, where `P_d` is the
   minimal period of right diagonal `d`. Because every `P_d` is a power of
   two, that depends only on `ord₂(p)` — which is crystal 12's mirror
   measurement, *"the white void touching the right edge at row `t` is a
   function of `ord₂(t)` alone"*, recorded there as **computed** with Cairn's
   values `0 2 3 5 6 8 14 15 23 24 26`. Proved here, and the values are
   reproduced and extended (`28`, `33`). Rowland proves the black-run sibling
   `I(t) = a(ord₂ t)` by right bijectivity; this is the white-gap one, from
   the tower.
3. **Nothing, for P1, and the reason is quantitative.** Read as a condition on
   the tower's free bits `R_k(0) = centerColumn k`, the cone condition at level
   `k` is a Boolean function of near-maximal algebraic degree — 16 of 17
   variables at `k = 17` — that is **affine in its top bit**. So the levels are
   independent, each spends exactly one free bit, and the whole family is
   equivalent to "the configuration is the seed". Having the separating
   property in the tower's language *is* having the centre column.

## 1. The residual, in one paragraph

Time runs down the picture. `centerColumn k` is the cell at the origin at row
`k`, and it is simultaneously index `0` of left diagonal `k` — where it sits
inside a transient nobody can bound — and index `0` of **right diagonal** `k`,
where the diagonal is exactly periodic with period `2^k` from its very first
cell and has no transient at all. That is why crystal 70 called the right edge
a better position for P1. Obstruction 8 then closed the position from one side:
the tower of right diagonals is generated by the recurrence from `R_0`, `R_1`
and one free bit per level, that free bit *is* the centre column, and the
configuration `…10101|000` — the `(01)^ℤ` fixed point meeting white at the
origin — shares with the seed its recurrence, its two edge diagonals and its
initial row at every `x ≥ 0` while having a flat tower of period 2 at every
depth. Its verdict: the property that separates the seed from that witness is
the existence of a **leftmost black cell**, and that "lives outside the tower".
The residual of P1 at this edge is therefore: find a statement about the right
diagonals that the seed satisfies and the flat tower does not, that is weak
enough to be a hypothesis rather than a restatement of the initial row, and
strong enough that a periodic centre column would contradict it. This document
writes the separating statement down, proves it, and shows it is not weak.

## 2. Why the known routes fail

**Obstruction 8 (the flat tower is a real picture).** No property of the
recurrence, the two edge diagonals, the initial row at `x ≥ 0`, or any
statistic of the right half decides whether the right-diagonal periods grow,
because `…10101|000` shares all of them. Amended here in its letter and
confirmed in its spirit: C2 and §4.

**Obstruction 17 (unbounded right-diagonal periods do not force an aperiodic
centre column).** The converse half: `b = (1000)^∞` gives a tower reaching
minimal period `2^27` by depth 64 with doubling density `0.406`, identical to
the seed's to three digits. So even a sharp theorem about the period growth
buys nothing for P1. Untouched here; this document is about the *cone*, not
about the periods — and C3 is precisely such a sharp theorem, which is why it
is filed as supply rather than as progress.

**Obstruction 7 (a universal argument over periodic words cannot give
minimality).** Its witnesses are arbitrary pairs of periodic words, and
obstruction 17's correction notes none is reachable as a consecutive pair in an
actual tower. Not engaged, and C3 sidesteps it: `edge_gap_eq` quantifies over
the *minimal* period directly rather than over the doubling criterion's
even-branch minimality, which obstruction 7 leaves open.

**Obstruction 1 (the diagonal structure never reaches the centre column).**
Its last sentence is the trap this topic aimed at: the left-diagonal recurrence
"closes on the very cell it was meant to explain". The right-side recurrence
closes the same way — `R_k(0)` is the free coordinate — and running it backwards
does **not** escape that (C5).

**Crystal 69 (the centre column outruns the settling front).** A fence on the
packed-row side; not engaged, since the right diagonals have no settling front.

**Obstructions 9 and 20 (no statistic of the half-line separates the seed from
an arbitrary boundary).** These are why C5 reads as a fence rather than a step:
the cone condition *is* a separating statement, and its price is that it is not
a statistic at all but a recursion that pins the boundary bit by bit.

**A route I considered and discarded on paper, before measuring.** Pair the
cone condition at level `k` with the doubling criterion at level `k+2`: both
are parities of the *same* driver `g_k(j) = R_{k-1}(j+1) | R_{k-2}(j+2)`, one
over the `k` indices at the end of a period (C4) and one over a whole period.
There are 21 doubling depths below `2^21` and no null model survives 21
samples; my own notebook records "a striking constant measured at one `n`" as
this project's most repeated error, so I did not measure it. Recorded so the
next session does not either without a bigger sample.

**New dead end, appended to `docs/obstructions.md`** as *"The cone condition
does live inside the right-diagonal tower, and having it is having the centre
column"*.

## 3. Candidate claims

### C1 — the right diagonals extend to negative index, and the periodicity extends with them

**The claim.** `rightDiagonal k j = evolve (j + k) j` makes sense for every
`j ≥ -k`, where at `j = -k` it reads the initial row at position `-k`; the
recurrence runs backwards there; and the period `2^k` holds on the **whole**
domain, not only from index 0. Statable with no new definition:

> for every `k` and every `i ≤ k`,
> `evolve (k - i) (-(i : ℤ)) = rightDiagonal k (2 ^ k - i)`.

The left side is the backward extension read as a cell of the picture; the
right side is a cell of the tower at a non-negative index. With a definition
(`rightDiagonalBack k i := evolve (k - i) (-(i : ℤ))`) it reads as periodicity
of one sequence on `j ≥ -k`.

**Band: nothing, as mathematics.** An induction over a recurrence that is
invertible in one argument. Its whole value is as the bridge that makes the
cone condition — definitionally a statement about the initial row — a statement
about the tower at indices the tower already has.

**Falsification.** `explorer/sextant10_backward.mjs`, seed's picture to row
3000.
- The backward recurrence `R_k(j+1) = R_k(j) ⊕ (R_{k-1}(j+1) | R_{k-2}(j+2))`:
  **1,441,198 instances, 0 failures**, of which **720,599 at negative `j`**,
  down to `j = -k` at every `k ≤ 1200`.
- Backward periodicity `R_k(-i) = R_k(2^k - i)`: the tower built forward from
  `R_0`, `R_1` and the free bits over `[0, 2^24)`, **300 pairs (`k ≤ 24`,
  `1 ≤ i ≤ k`), 0 mismatches**.
- `explorer/sextant10_family.mjs`: the same test over **eight configurations**
  — the seed, four random left-finite members of crystal 40's family, and three
  with periodic left tails including the flat tower — **8 × 210 pairs, 0
  failures**, 0 tower-vs-picture mismatches in every case.

*What else could have produced the survival.* Three things, each checked. (a)
The tower could be wrong, in which case both sides would be wrong together:
cross-checked against the picture at **10,025 cells, 0 mismatches**, and the
two sides are computed by independent routes (left from the picture, right from
the recurrence). (b) The padding: my first run reported **6 mismatches at
`k = 24`**, all mine — `cur[L-1]` has no honest driver and the corruption
creeps one index left per level, so with `L = 2^24 + 4` the corrupt zone
reached `2^24 - 24`. With slack it is 0. (c) It could hold for the seed
accidentally: hence the eight-configuration sweep, which includes three
configurations that **violate** the cone condition and still satisfy backward
periodicity — so the test is not measuring the cone by accident.

**Novelty.** New phrasing. Rowland 2006 lines 289–293 give the mechanism in
words — *"For right bijective rules, the periodicity is not eventual but
immediate. Intuitively, this is because periodicity in a column continues
uniquely backward in time"* — as the intuition for why his Lemma 2's
periodicity starts at index 0; but Lemma 2 is stated for `t ≥ 0` and proved by
pigeonhole on `f^{jp}R` with Lemma 1, not by a backward run, and the
negative-index segment down to the initial row is not an object he forms.
Searched `sources/` for `backward`, `negative index`, `negative indices`,
`extend backward`, `below index`: the only hit that bears on it is that
sentence (`spencer-2013` lines 985, 1030, 1108 are the leftward solve of a
*row*; `martinez-adamatzky` line 1141 is CA with memory).

**Route.** Induction on `k`. Base `k = 1`: the one negative index is `j = -1`,
and `R_1(-1) = X(-1) = b_1 ⊕ b_0 = R_1(1)` — one application of `rule30_eq` at
the origin, and it holds for **every** configuration white at `x ≥ 1`, which is
why C1 is family-wide. Step: `periodicFrom_mul` lifts levels `k-1`, `k-2` to
the common period `2^k` on their full domains, which makes the driver `g_k`
`2^k`-periodic on `j ≥ -k` (its reads are at `j+1 ≥ -(k-1)` and
`j+2 ≥ -(k-2)`, exactly the available range); then backward induction on `j`
from `R_k(2^k) = R_k(0)`. No hard step. Size M, with one definition.

### C2 — the cone condition, inside the tower, proved

**The claim.** For every `k ≥ 1`, `rightDiagonal k (2^k - k) = false`;
equivalently, for every `n` and every `1 ≤ d ≤ n`,
`evolve (2^n) ((2^n : ℤ) - d) = false`. By C1 this is exactly
`R_k(-k) = initialConfig(-k)`: the cone.

**Band: known as a statement, project-internal as a proof.** Rowland 2006
Theorem 1 (`f^{l(k)^n} R → R`, row `2^n` converges to the initial row) with the
quantitative form at §1 lines 123–126 — *"for `n ≥ 1` at least `n` consecutive
white cells, preceded by a black cell, follow the white left tail on row `2^n`…
at least `n+1` central cells of row `2^n` coincide with `I`"* — in his mirror
orientation. On this board it is the second ingredient of **crystal 11**, which
says of it: *"That second ingredient is Rowland's own induction, not a finite
check."* It is neither.

**What it would give.** The statement obstruction 8 says does not exist: one
about the tower at non-negative indices that the seed satisfies and
`…10101|000` does not. Measured, the flat tower's boundary values `R_k(-k)` are
`1010101010101…` against the seed's `1` then all `0`, and its tower values
`R_k(2^k - k)` are `010101010101010` for `k = 1..15`, so it satisfies the
condition at `k = 1` and **first fails at `k = 2`**. And the *eventual* form is
the interesting one: within crystal 40's family, `∃ m, ∀ k > m,
R_k(2^k - k) = false` is equivalent to `X` being white at every `x ≤ -m-1`,
i.e. to `X` being **number-like** — exactly the hypothesis of Jen's theorem and
Kopra's Theorem 3.5, now inside the tower. What remains after it is everything:
C5.

**Falsification.** *Proved*, not measured.
`explorer/sextant10_scratch_cone.lean`, accepted by `lake env lean`: `cone_row`
(at any row `p`), `cone_row_pow`, `cone_rightDiagonal`, `a_ge`, and the helper
`rightDiagonal_periodicFrom_pow_le`; axioms
`[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]` on each, within the allowlist. The
proof: suppose row `2^n` is black at `2^n - d` for some `1 ≤ d ≤ n`; take the
least such `d = m`; then `rightDiagonal_first_failure` (at `p = 2^n`) gives

### C3 — the edge gap is exactly the first depth whose period fails to divide the row

**The claim.** Proved, at any row.

> Let `P_d` be the minimal period of right diagonal `d`. If `P_d ∣ p` for every
> `d < D` and `P_D ∤ p`, then row `p` is white at every position `p - d` with
> `1 ≤ d < D`, and **black** at `p - D`.

So the nearest black cell to row `p`'s right edge sits at distance exactly
`min { d : P_d ∤ p }`. Every `P_d` is a power of two, so `P_d ∣ p` depends only
on `ord₂(p)`, and therefore **so does the gap**. Writing `a(n)` for the gap at
`p = 2^n`, the gap at any `p` is `a(ord₂ p)` and the white void at the right
edge is `a(ord₂ p) - 1`.

One honest restriction: the Lean statement is at `D + 2`, so it covers gaps of
`2` or more. `D = 0` cannot occur (`P_0 = 1` divides everything) and `D = 1` is
`P_1 = 2 ∤ p`, i.e. `p` odd, where the claim is that every odd row is black one
cell in from its right edge — which is the closed node
`evolve_right_second_diagonal` (`evolve (t+1) t = decide (t % 2 = 0)`, black
exactly when `t` is even, i.e. when `t + 1` is odd). So the identity is covered
at every `p`, by this theorem for `ord₂ p ≥ 1` and by that node for `p` odd.

**Band: known-adjacent, with a new proof, and it converts a crystal's
*computed* half into a theorem.** Rowland proves the black-run sibling — his
`I(t) = a(ord₂ t)`, §1 lines 117–126, "by right bijectivity". Crystal 12
records the white-void version as Cairn's measurement, *computed*, with the
values `0 2 3 5 6 8 14 15 23 24 26`. Those are now a theorem. The *identity
with the period spectrum* — that the gap is `min{d : P_d ∤ p}` — is the precise
two-way form of Rowland's line-131 assertion that `a(n)` "characterizes the
period lengths of the diagonals on the right side of rule 30"; his line is the
forward direction and is asserted in the introduction. I did not re-read his §3
this session, and my 2026-09-08 notebook records only the introduction
statement, so **treat "not proved in print" as unestablished** and the identity
as a new phrasing with a proof, not as a new theorem.

**What it would give.** Crystal 11 with room to spare — it wants `n` white
cells at row `2^n` and this proves `a(n) - 1 ≈ 2.39 n` of them — and crystal
12's mirror half outright. Structurally it says where the cone condition's
strength comes from: not from the cone but from the **minimal-period
spectrum**, so any improvement to the white run at row `p` is exactly an
improvement to a lower bound on the periods, and conversely. Both period-growth
theorems (`rightDiagonal_period_unbounded`, proved) therefore transfer to the
edge gap, and that is all the growth this gives.

**Falsification.** *Proved.* `explorer/sextant10_scratch_cone.lean`:
`cone_row`, `edge_black_within`, `antiperiodic_odd_mul`, `edge_gap`,
`rd_minimalPeriod_pow`, `rd_periodicFrom_minimalPeriod`, `rd_periodic_of_dvd`,

`edge_gap_eq` — all accepted, all axioms within the allowlist. The two halves:
`cone_row` gives the white run from periodicity; the black cell comes from the
**doubling**, because at the first depth `D` whose period fails to divide `p`
the two shallower depths have periods dividing `p`, so if the driver had even

`rightDiagonal_periodicFrom_step_of_even_driver` would make `P_D ∣ L ∣ p`, a

### C4 — the telescoped identity: the centre column is a parity along the backward line

**The claim.** Right diagonal `k` at index `-i` is the cell `(k-i, -i)`, so
running level `k` backwards walks the line `x = t - k` from the centre-column
cell `(k, 0)` up to the initial row at `(0, -k)`. Telescoping the rule along
that line, with the initial row white at `-k`:

> for every `k ≥ 1`,
> `centerColumn k = XOR over s < k of ( evolve s (s-k+1) OR evolve s (s-k+2) )`.

Each term is the OR of two **horizontally adjacent** cells of row `s`, so it is
`0` exactly at a white pair, and the identity reads
`centerColumn k ≡ k + #{white pairs along the line} (mod 2)`.

**Band: nothing.** The rule applied `k` times down one line. No tower, no
periodicity, no cone beyond `initialConfig (-k) = false`.

**What it would give.** The cone condition in the picture's own coordinates,
with `evolve` and nothing else, and the explicit recursion determining
`centerColumn k` from the rows above it. It is also the answer to the topic's
first death mode: the condition is **not** an identity forced by the recurrence
— the telescoping is, but the *value* `0` at the far end is the cone, and the
flat tower fails the identity at exactly the even `k` (20 of 40 tested).

**Falsification.** `explorer/sextant10_backward.mjs`: `k = 1..2500`, **0
failures**; the white-pair form at `k = 4, 8, …, 2048`, with the count of terms
forced white by the cone alone equal to `⌊(k-1)/2⌋` at every one. Kernel:
`backward_line_step` proves one step of the walk from `rule30_eq` (axioms
`[propext, Quot.sound]`), and a `decide` over a local fold `lineXor` verifies
the whole identity for `k = 1..14` against `rowCell`.

*What else could have produced the survival.* Nothing: it is an algebraic
identity, so the measurement checks my index arithmetic and not the automaton,
and I say so rather than reporting `0 failures over 2500 levels` as evidence
about rule 30. The one thing it does test is the sign of the cone term, which
is why the flat-tower half matters.

**Novelty.** Nearest in print: Rowland §5 (lines 841, 894–901) has parities of a
driver over a **period**, the doubling criterion, not along a line. The board's
`centerColumn_run_boundary` is the `k = 1` case. Portage's telescoped
centre-column sum (obstruction "Turn a measured obstruction into a theorem") is
the **left**-side analogue and was measured not to be a cocycle over the settled
part; this is the right-side one, with no settled/transient split at all.
Searched `sources/` for `telescop`, `parity of`, `XOR sum`, `running sum`,
`number of black cells in`.

**Route.** Induction on `s` from `backward_line_step`. The missing definition is
the fold: the board has no "XOR over a range", and the statement cannot be
written without one. Size M with the definition.

### C5 — as a condition on the free bits, it is affine in the top bit and of near-maximal degree

**The claim.** Within crystal 40's family write `F_k(b_0, …, b_k) := X_b(-k)`,
so the cone condition at level `k` is `F_k = 0`. Then, measured exactly:
- `F_k = b_k ⊕ G_k(b_0, …, b_{k-1})` — **affine in its top variable at every
  `k ≤ 17`**, so level `k` determines `b_k` from the levels below it;
- `deg F_k` is `1, 1, 2, 2, 4, 5, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16` for
  `k = 1..17`: **`≈ 0.87 k`, near-maximal**;
- the ANF support is `43,508` of the `2^18` monomials at `k = 17`, a density of
  **`0.166`, stable over `k = 9..17` (`0.156`–`0.171`, no trend)**, against
  `0.5` for a random function;
- the levels are **independent**: any subset `S` cuts the free bits by exactly
  `|S|`, and the full family `k = 1..K` leaves exactly **two** solutions — the
  all-white configuration and the seed.

**Band: novel as a measurement, and a fence rather than a step.** Nobody has
measured it. What it measures is that the topic's third death mode fires.

**What it would give.** The verdict on the topic. The condition is not trivially
satisfied (C2's flat-tower half; and a random `b` violates it at mean level
`2.0162` against a coin's `2.0`, with the whole histogram a coin's). It is not
merely the forward evolution restated cell for cell — it is a genuine recursion,
and a *sparse* subfamily of levels excludes the flat tower while leaving the
centre column massively underdetermined. But its degree grows linearly and its
ANF has about `2^k / 6` monomials, so no argument is going to use it. And the
fence is sharper than "unusable": because the levels are independent and each
spends one bit, **a cofinite subfamily determines the centre column from a
finite prefix of itself** — which is precisely "white far to the left", and
precisely why having the separating property in the tower's language is having
the centre column.

**The topic's question, answered.** Yes: the cone condition is a statement the
board can carry, it needs no new definition, and it is
`rightDiagonal k (2^k - k) = false`. Obstruction 8's verdict is wrong in its
letter — the separating property does **not** live outside the tower — and right
in its spirit, for a reason it did not state and I did not expect. The
condition is not a weak structural property of the tower that could be
leveraged against a periodic centre column. It is **one independent bit of
constraint per level, affine in exactly the free bit of that level**, so the
whole family is equivalent to "the configuration is the seed" and a cofinite
subfamily to "the configuration is number-like, with the centre column
determined by a finite prefix of itself". The residual at the right edge is
therefore unchanged in content and clarified in form: P1 asks whether a sequence
satisfying a specific nonlinear recursion — degree `≈ 0.87 k` at level `k`, ANF
density `1/6` — can be eventually periodic. Nothing about that shape is easier
than P1, and the degree is why.

One thing I am **not** claiming, and it is the temptation this document is
closest to: that the cone condition is useless. It is what crystals 11 and 12
need, it is exactly tight, and the right-diagonal region gains four or five
proved nodes and a printed theorem. What it does not gain is a handle on the
centre column.
