## 1. The residual, in one paragraph

Time runs down the picture. Read the seed's picture along the diagonals that run down and to the left, parallel to the left edge: diagonal `k` is the `k`-th one in from the edge. Each settles into a repeating word whose period is a power of two, and the period can only change at the diagonals that settle to all white: after an eventually-white diagonal the period either doubles or stays, and it never falls back once it has doubled (below `200,000` the eventually-white diagonals are `2, 7, 28, 399, 53207, 58286, 87866`; the period doubles at `3, 8, 29, 400, 87867` and stays at `53208` and `58287`). The wall says the period of diagonal `k` never exceeds `k + 1`. Since the period after the `n`-th doubling is `2^n`, that is exactly the statement that the `n`-th doubling happens at a diagonal `k_n ≥ 2^n - 1`: the doublings must keep spacing out at least geometrically. Measured, they space out far faster than that (`3, 8, 29, 400, 87867, 2107985255`), so the wall has enormous slack and no proof. The residual is therefore a lower bound on the gaps between eventually-white diagonals, and the topic asks what in the recurrence makes those gaps grow. The settled words obey the diagonal recurrence exactly, so the sequence of settled words is an orbit of a map on pairs of periodic words that never consults the picture except for one bit at each eventually-white diagonal; the question lives entirely in that orbit, in the settled region far to the left of the seam, and touches neither the transients nor the centre column. The answer this document reaches: the gaps grow because the white states are a fraction `2^-L` of the pairs the orbit walks through at period `L`, and the orbit never repeats a pair; that gives the measured law (gap about `2^L`) and a provable *upper* bound on the gaps, and it gives no lower bound at all, because the same finite system contains whites eight diagonals apart at every period.

## 2. Why the known routes fail

- **The settled part does not depend on the centre column** (obstructions, fourth entry) and **the left of the picture knows one integer** (fifth entry): the settled words are the recurrence's orbit from the edge with one bit per eventually-white diagonal, the same for every configuration white far to the left. For this wall that is not an obstruction but the setting: the wall is a statement about one universal orbit, and everything below is about that orbit.
- **The diagonal structure never reaches the centre column** (first entry) and the two boundary entries (second, third) and the two front entries (sixth): they are about the transient band and the centre; this wall never leaves the settled region, and none of them bears.
- **Rowland's doubling criterion** (crystals 13, his Proposition 2 and Lemma 3, lines 839–903 of the extraction): it says *when* the period doubles (an eventually-white diagonal whose outer driver has odd parity) and nothing about *how often*. Every attempt below is an attempt to say how often, and the criterion is the local law they all use.
- **A universal lower bound on the hitting time of the finite system** (new, appended today as the seventh obstruction entry): the natural way to prove "the next doubling is at least `L` diagonals away" is to prove it for every settled word the orbit could hold, since the orbit's word is unknowable; but for every even `L` from `8` to `128` there is a word of exact period `L` from which the next eventually-white diagonal is `8` steps away (C5 below). The bound can only hold for the seed's own words, and nothing distinguishes them.
- **Considered and not pursued:** an invariant of the words right after a doubling. Those words are antiperiodic (shifting by half the period complements them), and for `L ≤ 32` no antiperiodic word reaches a white within `2L + 8` steps; but the orbit loses every trace of the symmetry after seven steps (`orbitclass.mjs`, section 3 C3), and the minima over the antiperiodic words at `L = 8` and `16` are exactly what a geometric law gives for two and sixteen independent samples. There is no invariant to find there.

## 3. Candidate claims

Notation, used throughout. `D_k(j) = leftDiagonal k j`. `S_k` is the settled word of diagonal `k`, the periodic function on all of `ℤ` that `D_k` agrees with from its onset; on the board it is `D_k(j + 2^k)` for `j ≥ 0`. The recurrence `leftDiagonal_recurrence` holds on the settled words at every index, so `S_k` is the unique periodic solution driven by `S_{k-2}, S_{k-1}` whenever `S_{k-1}` has a black cell (the reset lemma is the uniqueness), and one of two when `S_{k-1}` is white. Write `L` for the current period. A *white* is a diagonal with `S_m = 0`. The *hitting time* `h(w)` of a word `w` is the number of diagonals from a white `m` whose successor word is `S_{m+1} = w` to the next white: `S_{m+h} = 0`, everything between determined by the recurrence. The seed's hitting times are `5, 21, 371` (from `2, 7, 28`), then `52808, 5079, 29580` (from `399, 53207, 58286`), then `1,420,791,102` and `687,106,286` (from `87866` and `1,420,878,968`, C4).

### C1. The local dictionary of a white: a diagonal is eventually white iff its two drivers are eventual shifts of each other, and what follows a white is forced for two steps

**The claim.** In English: an inner diagonal turns white for good at the first black cell of the diagonal just outside it once that diagonal reads, cell for cell, as the next one out shifted by one; conversely, past the onset of an eventually-white diagonal its two drivers are shifts of each other. After a white, the diagonal two further in is black for good from the first black cell of the diagonal between, and the one after that is the complement of the diagonal two out, shifted by one. Precisely, for all `m, N, j`:

- (A) `N ≤ j → (∀ i ≥ N, D_{m+1}(i+1) = D_m(i+2)) → D_{m+1}(j+1) = true → ∀ i ≥ j+1, D_{m+2}(i) = false`;
- (B) `(∀ i ≥ N, D_{m+2}(i) = false) → ∀ i ≥ N, D_m(i+2) = D_{m+1}(i+1)`;
- (C) `N ≤ j → (∀ i ≥ N, D_{m+1}(i) = false) → D_{m+2}(j+1) = true → ∀ i ≥ j+1, D_{m+3}(i) = true`;
- (D) `(∀ i ≥ N, D_{m+3}(i) = true) → ∀ i ≥ N, D_{m+4}(i+1) = !D_{m+2}(i+2)`.

In the orbit's own terms: white at `m` means `(S_{m-2}, S_{m-1}) = (u, σu)` with `σ` the shift by one; then `S_{m+1} = w` is the running xor of `σu` (the branch word), `S_{m+2} = 1`, `S_{m+3} = ¬σw`, and the period doubles iff `u` has odd parity.

**What it would give.** The vocabulary in which anything about the gaps has to be said on the board: "eventually white" becomes a relation between two consecutive settled words, and the step after a white is a computation. It would be cited by any statement of C2 or C3 as nodes. It says nothing about how far apart the whites are: it fixes three diagonals after a white and no more.

**Falsification.** Not falsifiable by the engine (each is two lines from the recurrence); checked instead on the seed's orbit: (C) and (D) hold after every one of the seven whites below `200,000` (`orbit32.mjs`, the "after every white diagonal" line), and (A)/(B) are what `settledwords.mjs` and `forbit.mjs` have used since 2026-09-07 to detect whites (a white found by the engine at `2, 7, 28, 399, 53207, 58286, 87866` and nowhere else to `200,000`, and the recurrence's shift test agreeing at every one). **Distrust:** the survival checks my indexing, not the lemmas; the first version of the script had (D) off by one index and reported "FAILS" seven times, which is what an honest index error looks like here.

**Novelty.** (C) is Rowland 2006, lines 905–908 ("column `m+1` is eventually black"), stated in words. (A), (B), (D) are new phrasing of `leftDiagonal_recurrence`; Rowland uses "column `m-1` eventually white" as a hypothesis (Proposition 2) and never characterises it. Searched rowland (both), kopra, jen, wolfram-1986 for "eventually white", "white stripe", "becomes white", "shift", "complement": nothing else.

**Route.** Done, in the kernel: `explorer/scratch_whitestep.lean`, accepted by `lake env lean` on 2026-09-08, `#print axioms` gives `propext, Quot.sound` for all four; imports only `LeftDiagonalRecurrence`. Each is `leftDiagonal_recurrence` at one index, `Nat.le_induction` for (A) and (C), and a two- or four-case `cases`. Four S nodes under `leftDiagonal_period_le`; the hard step was nowhere.

### C2. The average gap between whites is at most `2^L`, exactly: the segments of the pair graph are disjoint and there are `4^L` pairs

**The claim.** In English: take every word `w` of period dividing `L` other than all-white and all-black, start the recurrence from the state "white, then `w`", and count the diagonals to the next white; the sum of all these counts is at most `4^L`, so the average is below `2^L`, and no single one exceeds `4^L`. Precisely, on the finite system: nodes are pairs `(a, b)` of words of period dividing `L` (`4^L` nodes), the edge from `(a, b)` goes to `(b, c)` with `c` the periodic solution of `c(i+1) = a(i+2) xor (b(i+1) || c(i))` (unique when `b ≠ 0`, the two complements when `b = 0`); every node has in-degree exactly one, because `a(i+2) = c(i+1) xor (b(i+1) || c(i))` recovers `a` from `(b, c)` and, when `b = 0`, both complements have the same predecessor `(∂c, 0)`. So the segments from each `(0, w)` to the next node `(·, 0)` are pairwise disjoint, `Σ_w h(w) ≤ 4^L`, and `h(w) ≤ 4^L`. On the board, the piece about the seed: for every `a`, some diagonal `k ≤ 4^(2^a) + 1` has `∀ N, ¬PeriodicFrom (leftDiagonal k) (2^a) N`; that is, the `(a+1)`-th doubling happens by diagonal `4^(2^a) + 1`, and between the `n`-th and the `(n+1)`-th doubling there are at most `4^(2^n)` diagonals.

**What it would give.** The exact form of "why the gaps grow": the white states `(u, σu)` are `2^L` of the `4^L` pairs, the orbit never repeats a pair, and the average segment between whites is the whole space divided by the number of starts. It is an upper bound and the wall needs a lower one; it would be cited by nothing on the wall's side. It sharpens crystal 43 (`leftDiagonal_period_unbounded`, which gives infinitely many doublings and no rate) to a rate, and it explains the measured law below.

**Falsification.** `explorer/meansum.mjs`: the graph built explicitly for `L = 2, 4, 8` (`16, 256, 65536` pairs) has maximum in-degree `1` and exactly `4^L` edges, as the argument says. The exact sums `Σ_w h(w)` over all nonconstant `w`: `10` of `16` at `L = 2`, `238` of `256` at `L = 4`, `60,022` of `65,536` at `L = 8` (`91.6 %` of all pairs lie on a segment from a white to the next; the rest are on cycles no white reaches), and `4,293,693,734` of `4,294,967,296` at `L = 16` (`99.97 %`, all `65,534` words uncapped, `189` seconds): the bound is essentially tight, and the mean is `1.000 · 2^L` at `L = 16` (`1.25, 1.06, 0.92` at `L = 2, 4, 8`), with the largest single gap `638,072 = 9.7 · 2^L`. Over `400` random words at `L = 16` the median is `0.705` of the mean, which is the `ln 2` of a geometric law (`hitting.mjs`). The same graph argument predicts, by counting in- and out-degrees, that the words of exact period `L` with odd parity outnumber those with even parity by exactly `2^(L/2)`; verified `2, 4, 16, 256` at `L = 2, 4, 8, 16`. The seed's own gaps against `2^L`: `5/4, 21/16, 371/256, 52808/65536` for the first white after each doubling, then `5079` and `29580` at `L = 16` and `1.42 · 10^9` and `6.87 · 10^8` at `L = 32` against `4.29 · 10^9`: ratios `1.25, 1.31, 1.45, 0.81, 0.08, 0.45, 0.33, 0.16`. Whites of odd parity, which double the period, are `128` of `254` at `L = 8` and `206` of `400` at `L = 16`; the seed's are `6` of `9`. **Distrust:** the sum bound is a counting argument and cannot fail; what the engine tests is that my step function is the recurrence (it reproduces the seed's `5, 21` by hand and agrees with `settledwords.F` on `112,000` consecutive words, `orbit32.mjs`). The "geometric law" is a fit to the histogram, not a claim: the small hitting times are not geometric at all, they come in structured families (`h = 5, 7, 8, 13, 16, 21, 29, 32, 41, 57, 58` are the only values below `64` at `L = 16`, with `88` words at `h = 8`), and the seed's `0.08` at `53207` is one draw.

**Novelty.** Nearest in print: Wolfram 1986 §6, lines 691–705, on the finite automaton of depth `N` from the edge, "in a first approximation, they can be considered uniformly distributed among possible `N`-site configurations, and their periods increase very slowly with `N`", and "the total number of cycle states thus increases at most quadratically with `N`". His system is the strip of depth `N`, not the pair of settled words at period `L`, and he gives no rate for the doublings. Rowland 2006 gives the criterion and the numbers `53209, 58288, 72577`. NKS p. 871 (not held) lists the doublings. The pair-graph counting and the bound `4^(2^n)` on the gap are not in anything held; the qualitative fact is crystal 43. Searched all sources for "hitting", "doubly exponential", "2^(2^", "gap": nothing.

**Route.** For the board statement: pigeonhole over `4^(2^a) + 2` consecutive diagonals `k ≤ 4^(2^a) + 1`, each read at an onset that is a multiple of `2^a` (as `leftDiagonal_period_unbounded`'s proof already does with `Finite.exists_ne_map_eq_of_infinite`), two consecutive pairs of residue words agree, and `leftDiagonal_pair_never_eventually_shifted` refuses. Size L, the same phase alignment as crystal 43's proof plus an explicit `Fintype.card` bound. The finite-system statement needs a definition the board does not have: the periodic solution map on `Fin L → Bool`, or on `ℕ → Bool` with `PeriodicFrom`. No hard step; low value.

### C3. From the word a doubling produces, the first doubling after it is at least `L` diagonals away, on every branch (the universal cousin of the wall)

**The claim.** In English: the word right after a doubling to period `L` is antiperiodic (its shift by `L/2` is its complement), because it is the running xor of a word of period `L/2` with odd parity; start the recurrence from "white, then any antiperiodic `w`", follow both continuations at every complement-type white, and the first odd-parity white is at least `L` diagonals away. Call the minimum over all antiperiodic `w` and all branches `T(L)`. Precisely: `∀ n ≥ 1, T(2^n) ≥ 2^n`. With `k_1 = 3` this gives `k_n + 1 ≥ 2^n` by induction (`k_{n+1} - k_n ≥ T(2^n)`), which is `leftDiagonal_period_le` once the period of diagonal `k` is known to divide `2^(number of doublings ≤ k)`, Rowland's criterion.

**What it would give.** The wall, by an induction whose every step is a statement about a finite system: for each `n`, `T(2^n) ≥ 2^n` is decidable by enumerating `2^(2^(n-1))` words. It would be cited by the wall directly. What would remain is the `∀ n`, which is the whole difficulty: see the route.

**Falsification.** `explorer/hitting2.mjs`, every antiperiodic word at `L = 2, 4, 8, 16, 32` (`2, 4, 16, 256, 65536` words) with a cap of `2L + 8` steps and both branches followed: `T(2) = 5`, and no white of any kind within the cap for `L ≥ 4`, so `T(L) > 2L + 8 ≥ L` for `L ≤ 32`. *Survives* to `L = 32`. Uncapped (`hitting3.mjs`): the first white from an antiperiodic word is at least `88` at `L = 8` (two shift classes, `88` and the seed's `371`) and at least `6343` at `L = 16` (sixteen classes; the seed's `52808` is the median), and at `L = 32` exactly one of the `2048` classes reaches a white below `2^20`, at `414,989` (`11` minutes; the script now caps at `2^18` and states the verdict in its header). **Distrust, and this is the point:** the survival is what a coin gives. The `L` shifts of a word share one hitting time, so the independent samples are the shift classes, `2, 16, 2048` of them at `L = 8, 16, 32`, and the minimum of `C` geometric samples with mean `2^L` sits near `2^L / C`: `128, 4096, 2 · 10^6` against the observed `88, 6343, 414989` (the last a single class below the cap where the null expects half a class). The probability that a coin passes the `T(2^n) ≥ 2^n` test at level `n` is about `1 - C · L / 2^L = 1 - 2^(-L/2)` with `L = 2^n` (each of the `C` classes fails with probability `L / 2^L`), so a coin passes every level from `n = 3` on with probability above `0.93`, and passes `n = 1, 2` because those two systems are tiny and fully known. I looked for structure that would make the survival more than a coin and found none: the orbit after a doubling reads `0, w, 1, ¬σw`, then three generic words, then one more antiperiodic word, then nothing (`orbitclass.mjs`: in the period-`16` stretch `400..87866`, `343` shift-invariant and `295` antiperiodic words among `87,467`, against `683` expected for random words; the first unsymmetric word is always the fourth after the doubling).

**Novelty.** Not in print; the wall in another coat, as crystals says of the left half-line conjecture. Its content over the wall is only that it is universal over starting words, which C5 shows is exactly what makes the corresponding statement for *all* words false.

**Route.** No route. A proof would need a property of antiperiodic starting words that survives `L` steps of a recurrence that forgets its start in seven; the enumeration is the only evidence and it is evidence of a coin. Do not seed it as a node.

### C4. The seed's orbit at period `32`: the next white is at `1,420,878,968`, complement type; NKS's `2,107,985,255` is the sixth doubling, on the continuation the seed takes

**The claim.** In English: run the settled-word recurrence from the words at diagonals `199,999` and `200,000` with no further input; the first white diagonal is `1,420,878,968`, its outer driver has even parity so the period stays `32` and the two continuations are complements; on one of them the next white is `2,107,985,254` with odd parity, so the period doubles to `64` at `2,107,985,255`; on the other the next whites are `3,340,408,059` and `4,989,445,007`, both of complement type, with no doubling before `5.16 · 10^9`. Precisely, in the board's terms: `∀ N, PeriodicFrom (leftDiagonal 1420878968) 1 N` is false, but `leftDiagonal 1420878968 j = false` for all `j` past its onset (it is eventually white); and the first `k` with `∀ N, ¬PeriodicFrom (leftDiagonal k) 32 N` is `2,107,985,255`.

**What it would give.** Nothing for the wall except slack: the wall now holds for every `k < 2^31 - 1` on the strength of the computation (period at most `32` below `2,107,985,255`, and `32 ≤ k + 1` from `k = 31` on; below `31` the periods are `1, 1, 1, 2, 1, 2, 2, 1, 4, …` and the bound holds by inspection). For Dib: the eighth eventually-white diagonal of the seed and the confirmation that NKS's figure is exact are, as far as the held sources go, new.

**Falsification.** `explorer/orbit32.mjs`, `8 · 10^9` steps, `140` seconds. The chain of trust: the slow orbit (`settledwords.F`) with the seven branch bits agrees with the engine's windows (rows to `137,000`) at every branch point and at `2400, 53210, 87868`; the bit-parallel step agrees with the slow orbit on all `112,133` words from `87,868` to `200,000` and with the bit-serial solve on `200,000` random pairs; the tree then runs the bit-parallel step alone. *Survives* in the sense that the computation ran to the stated depth; the picture cannot check any of it (the row that settles diagonal `1.4 · 10^9` is beyond `1.9 · 10^9`, with `4 · 10^9` cells). **Distrust:** what else could produce `2,107,985,255` besides its being the sixth doubling? A wrong branch bit at `53208` or `58287` would put the orbit on another of Rowland's left sides and the numbers would be other numbers; a wrong step function would give an orbit unrelated to the seed's; in either case matching a nine-digit figure that Wolfram computed from the picture by other means has probability of order `10^-9`. The one thing the match does not check is the seed's choice at `1,420,878,968` itself, which is *inferred* from the match: I have no reading of the picture there. The "or more" in the crystals' quotation of NKS could mean Wolfram's method gave only a bound, in which case this is the first exact determination; I do not hold NKS and cannot say. The other branch's whites (`3.34 · 10^9`, `4.99 · 10^9`) are properties of the recurrence's tree, not of the seed, exactly as Rowland's `72577` is.

**Novelty.** Rowland 2006 lines 936–946 gives the tree to `58288` and `72577` (our `58287`, `72576`); NKS p. 871, quoted in crystals 14 and 43, gives `2,107,985,255` for the sixth doubling; no source held mentions `1,420,878,968` or any white between `87866` and the sixth doubling. The method, the recurrence alone with branch bits read from the picture, is `forbit.mjs` (2026-09-07) pushed four orders of magnitude further by the observation that at period `32` a settled word is a machine integer and the periodic solution is a fixed point reached in at most `32` bit-parallel iterations.

**Route.** None as a theorem: `2 · 10^9` recurrence steps are not a kernel computation, and `native_decide` is outside the allowlist.

### C5. The eight-step family: at every even period `L ≥ 8` there is a word from which the next white is eight diagonals away, so no universal lower bound on the gaps exists

**The claim.** In English: the word "all black except the pattern `00100` at the end", of exact period `L`, started after a white, returns to a white in exactly eight diagonals, for every even `L` from `8` to `128`; its orbit at `L = 16` is `0 → 1^11 00100 → 1^16 → 0^10 110110 → (01)^5 001001 → 1^9 0110111 → (01)^4 00100101 → (10)^4 01001010 → 0`, the same words with longer constant and alternating runs at every other `L`. So the statement "from every word of period `L` the next white is at least `f(L)` away" is false for every `f` that exceeds `8`, and the wall cannot be proved from a bound that holds for all words the orbit could hold.

**What it would give.** A dead end with a witness: any proof of the wall must use something about the seed's own words at the whites, not only their period. It is the content of the seventh obstruction entry.

**Falsification.** This is the claim that *kills* the universal route, and it survived every attempt to kill *it*: `hitting.mjs` found the family exhaustively (`8` words at `h = 8` of exact period `8`; `88` at `L = 16`, of which the family is five shift classes; one in `200,000` random words at `L = 32` where a geometric law predicts `0.0015`), `hitting2.mjs` confirms `h = 8` for every even `L` from `8` to `32` with the bit-serial step and for every even `L` from `34` to `128` with an independent array implementation, and the family fails at every odd `L` (the orbit never returns to white within `64` steps), which is what a run-length mechanism does and a bug does not. Minimum over *all* nonconstant words is `5` at every `L` (the seed's own `(01)`, which has period `2` at every `L`); over words of exact period `L` it is `8` from `L = 8` on. **Distrust:** the words in the family are legal states of the finite system, and the wall is about the seed's orbit; nothing says the seed ever holds such a word. That is exactly the obstruction: nothing says it does not, either, and the density argument of C2 puts the chance per white at about `2^-L`.

**Novelty.** Not in print. Wolfram 1986 §6 says the finite automaton "always yields cycles" of small period; nothing on short returns to a white diagonal.

**Route.** A proof for all even `L` would be an induction on the run lengths through eight explicit steps; not worth a node, the witness is the point.

## 4. What survived

C1 survived and is in the kernel: four S-sized lemmas that make "eventually white" a relation between consecutive settled words and fix the two diagonals after a white; a captain can seed them under `leftDiagonal_period_le` as vocabulary, knowing they do not move the wall. C2 survived as a counting fact with a provable board form, the upper bound `4^(2^n)` on the gap between doublings, which is the honest answer to the topic's question "why do the gaps grow": the whites are a `2^-L` fraction of a space the orbit walks without repetition, and the measured gaps (`5, 21, 371, 52808, 1.42 · 10^9`) sit within a factor `3` of `2^L` as that predicts. C4 survived as a computation and is the session's finding for Dib. C3, the statement that would actually prove the wall, survived its enumeration to `L = 32` in the way a coin survives it, and I would not seed it. Nothing here gives a lower bound on a gap, and I now think nothing in the recurrence does: the period wall is true for the same reason the onset wall is, a small probability summed over the levels, and a proof would need to control the seed's specific words at the whites. I would seed C1 first, because it costs nothing and any later statement about whites needs it, and C2's board form second if a prover wants an L-sized exercise in pigeonhole; neither is under the wall in the sense of implying it.

## 5. Claims that died

- *"From every nonconstant word of period `L` the next white is at least `L` diagonals away."* Died at `L = 8`: `w = 11100100` (exact period `8`) returns to white in `8`; at `L = 16`, `96` words of exact period `16` have `h ≤ 16` (`hitting.mjs`). The family `1^(L-5) 00100` has `h = 8` for every even `L` from `8` to `128` (`hitting2.mjs`).
