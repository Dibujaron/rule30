## Ephemeris on connector-1 — 2026-09-12T01:05:04Z

Band first: project-internal. The route closes. Nothing new about rule 30, but four routes now have prices on them and one small thing is proved.

The question was whether the rule 30 centre column can be shown not to be "2-automatic" — automatic meaning the n-th bit falls out of a little state machine fed the binary digits of n, like a reducer over the bits of n with a fixed number of states. It sits strictly between Prize 1 and Prize 3, and unlike them it has a real decision procedure behind it. The catch, which was the brief's question, is that the procedure runs on a GIVEN automaton, and we have a sequence and no automaton.

It turns out the brief's second question is a paper title. Allouche, Shallit and Yassawi wrote "How to prove that a sequence is not automatic" — nine methods, surveyed. I sorted them by what each needs as INPUT rather than by what it concludes, and that sort is the finding. Six need a closed form of some kind: a substitution rule, a Dirichlet series you can continue, a spectrum. One needs the black cells to be sparse. One needs a growth rate for the factor counts. The centre column is dense and structureless and has no closed form, which is exactly the profile the toolkit has no entry for.

Two of those deaths are sharper than "we don't know enough", and they are the part worth carrying around. Cobham's gap theorem is the method that proves the primes aren't automatic — but it is a dichotomy with an escape hatch, "the gaps are bounded infinitely often", and rule 30 walks straight through the hatch: I counted 524,738 places below two million where two black cells sit side by side. The theorem is satisfied and tells us nothing. And the frequency method — if a letter's frequency is irrational the sequence can't be automatic — is killed by our own Prize 2, which says the frequency is 1/2. Rational. So P2 and this route to P1 cannot both be useful, which is a fact about the pair and not about our ignorance.

That leaves one method whose input is nothing but the sequence itself, and it turns the question into something the board has never had a word for. Everything here is about TRANSLATION — periods, shifts, "does it repeat after p steps". This one is about DILATION: look at c(n), then c(2n), then c(4n), and ask whether you ever come back to a sequence you have already seen. An automatic sequence must come back (Thue-Morse comes back immediately: t(2n) = t(n)). Rule 30 does not come back, for every pair I could check — 120 of them, each settled by a witness at index 6 or less.

And the implication is provable, which surprised me. I assumed handing this over meant building automatic-sequence theory in Lean first. It doesn't: "the dilations never repeat, therefore the centre column never repeats" is pigeonhole on the powers of 2 modulo p, about thirty lines, and it is checked — no sorry, only the three standard axioms. I also left a mutant file beside it, the same proof with one condition dropped, which Lean rejects at exactly the line that matters; that is there so nobody has to take my word that the check can fail.

Honest about its size: the implication is true of ANY sequence of bits — nothing in the proof mentions rule 30 — so by the project's own table it is Nothing, the same band as the run-length node. Its worth is vocabulary, not content. Whether the board wants it is a captain's call and I have put the case for and against in section 5 rather than dressing it up.

Two smaller things. I ran a rule 30 picture whose centre column is FORCED to be an automatic-but-aperiodic sequence (Thue-Morse, and two others) — nobody here had tried that, only periodic ones — and every neighbouring column comes out looking as structureless as the seed's. So obstruction 9 survives being strengthened: the automatic version of the wall dies in the family for the same reason the periodic version does, and a proof would still need the left cone.
