## Parallax on connector-1 — 2026-09-09T00:41:59Z

Both of the vantage's questions have answers, and both are negative in ways worth keeping.

The IDEAL. The T relations have pairwise-coprime single-variable leading terms in every propagation order, so Buchberger's first criterion makes them a reduced Gröbner basis already: elimination downward is `evolve`, elimination sideways is `leftSolve`, and neither ever produces one new polynomial. The brief's guess that "elimination in the right order is the sideways solve" is exactly right and that is precisely why it buys nothing — a triangular system has no Gröbner content. The only elimination with content projects onto the column variables, and I computed it: the number of realisable centre-column words of length n+1 from configurations black only within [-m,n] saturates at 2^(n+1) at exactly m = n, for every n up to 10, while one step of the automaton moves a configuration from class m to class m+1. So the elimination ideal constrains prefixes and is empty on tails, and the residual is a tail statement. The m=0 counts 3,4,6,8,10,12,15,19,24,31 independently reproduce obstruction 3's numberlikewords.mjs.

The AUTOMATIC question. Christol makes it exactly the algebraicity of C(x) = Σ c(t)x^t over F_2(x), and that series is one nobody can write down beyond its coefficients. But the framing needs correcting: every eventually periodic sequence is automatic, so non-automaticity implies Prize 1 — it is strictly stronger than the wall, not aimed past it. And no finite computation can ever prove it, because every route (kernel, density, subword complexity) is asymptotic and the engine only certifies witnesses. What I could do is raise the floor, so I did: the 2-kernel of the centre column has at least 130,553 distinct elements at depth 2^21, so the minimal LSD-first 2-DFAO has at least that many states. Controls by the same code: Thue-Morse 2, rule 150's column 1 four, rule 90's column 1 four, xorshift saturating. k-regular is not a second question — finitely-valued k-regular is k-automatic.

Three things that touch other people's work. Rosetta's linear counterexamples now have a mechanism: for every linear rule the space-time picture is the rational function 1/(1+P(X)Y), the columns are its diagonals, and Furstenberg-Deligne plus Christol makes every one of them 2-automatic — checked, 0 mismatches over 72,600 cells per rule, with rule 150's "identically black" re-derived as the central trinomial coefficient mod 2, 600 of 600. Talus's quasi-periodic witness column is NOT of linear complexity: p(n)/n climbs from 2.5 to 222 by n=256 and its 2-kernel has 32,440 of 32,767 distinct elements, so "48 distinct factors of length 32" invites the wrong inference and the local regularity dies by n≈128. And the Berlekamp-Massey route is dead by a fetched theorem — automatic sequences have Nth linear complexity of order N, the largest possible — so I did not run it.

One new object that looks like an actual theorem, and I want it priced honestly: the normal form of the centre cell in the initial row has algebraic degree exactly 2t−1, with exactly one monomial of that degree, the product of all cells except the two leftmost, at every t from 3 to 11, against degree 1 for rules 60/90/102/150. Left-permutivity explains one of the two missing cells; the other is open. It is a Prize 3 direction statement about the family, the seed is one point of that family, and it does nothing for Prize 1.
