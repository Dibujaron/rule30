Both numbers are gone, and one of them went in a way you can hold in your hand.

The 16 in `leftDiagonal_onset_le_of_le_5000` is not a bounded return period the wall forgot to ask for. It is 2^5, and the 5 counts how many times the left diagonals' period has doubled by depth 5000. NKS p. 871 lists the doublings at 3, 8, 29, 400, 87867 — so the least constant that closes depth k is a staircase 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 stepping at exactly those k. I computed the staircase from the recurrence alone for k = 0..600, and it stepped at 3, 8, 29, 400 with nothing in between. Then I went and looked at the fifth step directly: at k = 87866 the constant 16 still works, and at k = 87867 it does not — not "settles late", it has not settled at all by row 2k+132, while 32 settled 58,000 rows earlier. The kernel confirms the step at k = 400 in four `decide +kernel` calls. So: 16 is a coincidence of the tested range, with an exact expiry date, and `..._of_le_87866` is the last theorem that can use it.

The 4 in `stepMod_preperiod_le_of_le_11` is the same staircase read at k <= 11, where the orbit of 1 also has period 4. Nothing is closing faster than anything. Over a common range the two constants are equal, and exhaustively — the whole functional graph of the map on 2^n states, up to n = 24 — the largest cycle anywhere in the graph IS the orbit of 1's cycle. So quantifying over every start buys no bigger period at all. What it costs is time: the worst start's preperiod is 36 where the seed's is 30, at n = 28. The all-starts route is strictly stronger, never cheaper.

The thing I did not go looking for. Rowland's 2006 paper ends by saying that the left side of rule 30 first *could* branch at his column 53209, and then hedges: the second period would be a counterexample "if in fact they do occur for some initial conditions". Twenty years later that parenthesis is still a parenthesis. It occurs. At an eventually-white diagonal the next diagonal is a running XOR, so its complement is also a solution — flip that one bit in a cycle state and the flip never heals. Do it at the two whites where the period doubles (399, 87866) and you land back on rule 30's own left side at a different phase, which is Rowland's own "invariant under negation" case. Do it at the two where it does not (53207, 58286) and you land on a genuinely different left side. And that other left side's next white diagonal comes out at bit 72575 — Rowland's column 72577, the number he predicted in 2006 for exactly this branch. My engine had no way to know it.

So crystal 49's "there is only one left side of rule 30" is not a uniqueness for want of an alternative. The alternative exists and I have read its structure. What crystal 49 describes is a basin, and it is deep: 140 random rightful rows at widths past the branch all took rule 30's own side, none the other.
