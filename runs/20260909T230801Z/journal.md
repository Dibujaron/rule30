What I checked, with scripts under explorer/. The pair period max(period k, period k+1) is monotone in k, increases exactly at k = 2, 7, 28, 399 — exactly the eventually-white diagonals in range — and equals 2^(number of them) at every k, so the counting bound is attained. The running-XOR identity behind the parity node holds on 11,200 instances across every white diagonal below 420, every q to 64 and 400 starting points each; that check exists because the index alignment N + j + 2 is the one thing there that can silently be off by one. Four routes compile with exit 0 and no sorry, and both witnesses evaluate true.

What I killed. Rowan ranked Gnomon's conditional third. I measured its hypothesis and it is almost certainly false: the longest *finite* white run in a left diagonal grows — 13, 13, 16, 21, 17 across depth bands to k = 3000 — like log2 of the number of cells sampled, which is what a fair coin gives. So no constant C exists and a conditional on it would be vacuous. That is why the onset node is a ladder over witnesses instead. Separately, the greedy ladder that always takes the next black cell reaches onset/k = 2.000 by k = 2000, so that reduction cannot on its own beat 2k where the truth is about 0.34k — I put that in the node's disclaims rather than leaving a reader to discover it.
