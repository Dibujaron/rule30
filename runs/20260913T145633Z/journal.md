## Astrolabe on connector-1 — 2026-09-13T15:28:36Z

Band first: **project-internal**, with two measured rows that are novel-and-small.

The vantage was algebraic circuit complexity — the branch of complexity theory whose objects are polynomial families rather than programs, and whose lower-bound technology (the degree bound, Baur–Strassen, Nisan–Wigderson partial derivatives, the shifted partial derivatives of Gupta–Kamath–Kayal–Saptharishi) proves things unconditionally about one named family without separating any complexity classes. The board already owns the family: the centre cell at time t is a polynomial over the two-element field in the 2t+1 initial cells the light cone allows, and its degree is exactly 2t−1.

It is closed, and the reason is arithmetic rather than a missing theorem. Computing that one cell from a free initial row needs at least 2t operations, because all 2t+1 cells genuinely matter (measured exactly at every t ≤ 10), and at most 4t², because that is the simulation. Wolfram's third prize asks for a bound of shape Ω(t) — which is the *bottom* of that interval and is already proved by counting inputs — while the field's best lower bound for any explicit polynomial whatever is Ω(n log n), thirty years old. So the window in which an algebraic result could be news is one log factor wide, nobody has entered it, and none of it is about the prize's sequence anyway: the prize evaluates the whole family at a single fixed point, which is the non-uniformity trap docs/prize.md already records for Boolean circuits, arriving here in a worse form.

Four measures were run on the polynomial and all four put rule 30 in the middle of the elementary-rule pack. Its degree is two short of maximal (34 of the 256 rules are maximal); its ANF density ranks 56th; its Raz rank 37th, with 36 rules above it. The controls the brief asked for do fire — the linear rules 90 and 150 sit at degree 1 and rank 2 — but they are the only separation, and rules 22, 97 and 121 beat rule 30 on everything.

Two things survived to section 5, and the better one was an accident. The matrix I was computing for Raz's multilinear-formula lower bound is, up to an invertible change of basis on each side, the *communication matrix* of the cone map — verified rather than asserted, equal rank at 20 of 20 cells. Split row 0 at the origin, the project's own half-line split: the rank is about 2.4·2^(t/2), flat over t = 6…11, so the left half of row 0 cannot tell the origin what it needs in fewer than roughly t/2 bits, against a trivial upper bound of t+1. That is the only statement in the document with both a rate linear in t and a mechanism. The other is a fence: prove the rank is *never* full and the multilinear route closes for good.

What died: the Raz route on its own hypothesis (0 of 30 partitions full rank at any t ≤ 11, 0 of 256 rules at t = 7, while two nulls — one matched on monomial count — are full everywhere); shifted partial derivatives on their regime (the method's hard family has degree about the square root of its variable count, ours has degree = variables − 2); Baur–Strassen twice over (its direction is an upper bound, so it says the whole damage front is as cheap as the one cell it was meant to bound — and in characteristic two its formal gradient is not the sensitivity vector at all, agreeing at 3 of 2000 points at t = 10, with a multilinear control at zero disagreements); and geometric complexity theory for want of a symmetry group, the cone polynomial having exactly one, the mirror. Linear complexity and Christol's theorem were not re-chased: both are already on this board and closed, and section 4 says where.
