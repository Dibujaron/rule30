Dib — fifth sighting, on P1 from arithmetic dynamics and cycle exclusion. Band: Nothing to project-internal. No new fact about rule 30; what the session produces is a fence with a mechanism.

The question I set myself yesterday was whether any technique that excludes cycles of an arithmetic map survives replacing + by XOR and * by OR, and I said the guess (no) was only worth a document if the answer named what the nonlinearity costs. It does, in one sentence: the nonlinearity costs the RING. Steiner's proof that the 3x+1 map has no 1-cycle needs two things in the same ring — the composite map has one multiplier, and the ring's units have logarithms so Baker's theorem can bound a linear form. Rule 30's row map is polynomial over F_2, whose unit group has exactly one element, so there are no logarithms at all; the rings that do have them are ones where XOR is not even additive (measured: T(a+b) = T(a)+T(b)-T(0) fails 3730 of 4096 times). Steps 1 and 3 cannot be had together, and that is not a missing hypothesis anyone can supply.

Fields sighted: cycle exclusion for 3x+1 (Steiner, Simons, Simons-de Weger, Hercher), Baker, Yu's p-adic analogue, Call-Silverman canonical heights, Skolem-Mahler-Lech in both characteristics, Diophantine approximation, S-unit equations, matrix cocycles, Morton-Silverman, T-functions, Adamczewski-Bugeaud, Eliahou, and Kopra's powers-of-a-rational — fourteen rows. Six survived to a dictionary in section 5's sense; all six die, and section 4 is long.

Three results worth your attention. First, the literal transport is a category error and the measurement is one line: rowNat t has exactly 2t+1 binary digits, so the orbit is strictly increasing, so "this orbit is not periodic" — the conclusion of every theorem in that literature — is two lines here and is not the prize. The prize is about a single digit read at a position that moves one place per step, and the 3x+1 literature contains no theorem about any digit of any trajectory. Second, the multiplier: over all 2^n points, every linear rule has exactly ONE distinct Jacobian (so its composite derivative is a single matrix power, which is what makes Baker applicable), and rule 30 has exactly 2^n — the derivative determines the point, the precise opposite of one fixed number. Third, and the one that surprised me: the step I expected to be the obstruction is already finished. Write log2(rowNat t) = 2t + theta_t; theta_t is the orbit's entire archimedean size, the thing that is the whole difficulty in the 3x+1 problem, and it is purely periodic to every fixed precision with no transient — because the top digits of the row are the right diagonals, which Rowland proved periodic. So that step is closed and the prize survives it untouched. Any future vantage proposing to control rule 30 by controlling its orbit's size is refuted in advance.
