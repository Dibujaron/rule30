Dib — Sextant again, same wall, second sighting. I went after the pair of columns 0 and 1 and what the cone actually forces on them, and this time I could write scripts, so the fixes are from the engine and the kernel rather than from my hand.

What I attacked. The idea that the pair is a finite counting problem: fix a periodic centre column, count how many column-1 prefixes (or right halves) are consistent with the cone, hope it stays bounded so column 1 is forced. That was my own suggestion last time. It is dead, and the reason is worth knowing: the count doubles nearly every step for any column that survives at all, because a black cell placed to the right cannot change the centre until the damage front from it has walked left, at the measured 0.24 cells per row. For the true centre column the count goes 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, … 215264 at depth 21. I wrote it into docs/obstructions.md so nobody proposes it again.

What survived, and how deep. The useful thing the cone does to the pair is simpler than a count. The rule at the origin reads c(t+1) = L(t) xor (c(t) or R(t)), with L the column just left and R the column just right. Whenever the centre is black, R vanishes from that formula and c(t+1) is the complement of L(t). And L is computed by the left half-line from column 0 alone. So a periodic centre column has to pass a test at every one of its black times that never looks at the right half. I ran that test on every periodic boundary of period up to 20, on single-pulse boundaries to period 240, on two-pulse boundaries to period 40, with short left words: every one fails, the deepest after 21 tests, and the pass rate per test is exactly 0.50. Read that honestly: the boundaries fail the way a coin fails, and the sweep would not recognise a structured survivor at large period even if one existed. The kernel accepted the small case by decide: no configuration white on x ≤ -3 has a centre column of period at most 5, whatever its right half. That is the first statement anyone here has checked about all of Kopra's number-like configurations rather than the single seed.

Two more that held: column 0 and the right half are free coordinates on configuration space (given a column and a right half there is exactly one configuration; Wolfram said the counting half in 1986, the uniqueness is one board lemma), and the onset "discrepancy" I flagged last time was me: onsets are 0..3 up to diagonal 23 and then climb to about 0.4k, both numbers right.

What I would seed: the two definitions (sideways solve, left half-line), the split identity, and the finite exclusion. Not the conjecture itself; it is the wall in a different coat.
