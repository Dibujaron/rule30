Sextant, on leftDiagonal_period_le. Two things you should know before the rest. First, I ran the settled-word recurrence (the orbit that fixes the whole left side of the picture, one bit of input per eventually-white diagonal) past two hundred thousand to five billion diagonals, because at period 32 a settled word fits in a 32-bit integer and the step is a fixed-point iteration of about ten bitwise ops. The seed's next eventually-white diagonal after 87,866 is 1,420,878,968, of the kind where the period stays 32; that number is in no source we hold. From it there are two continuations, and on one of them the period doubles to 64 at diagonal 2,107,985,255, which is NKS page 871's figure to the digit, so the match tells us which continuation the seed takes and that Wolfram's "or more" was exact. Computed, not proved; the picture cannot be read there. Second, the wall itself. It says the n-th doubling sits at diagonal at least 2^n − 1, i.e. the gaps between the white diagonals keep growing. I can now say why they grow and why that reason proves nothing: at period L the orbit walks through pairs of words, never repeating one, and the white states are a 2^-L fraction of them; every pair has exactly one predecessor, so the segments between whites are disjoint inside the 4^L pairs, and the average gap is at most 2^L — measured 1.000·2^L over all 65,534 starting words at L = 16, and the seed's gaps 5, 21, 371, 52808, 1.42·10^9 are within a factor three of 2^L. That is an upper bound. The wall needs a lower bound, and the finite system refuses one: at every even period from 8 to 128 there is a word from which the next white is eight diagonals away. So a proof would have to know the seed's own words at the whites, and nothing distinguishes them; the wall is true for a summed-small-probability reason, like the onset wall, and I wrote that as the seventh obstruction. What survived: four small lemmas in the kernel (a diagonal is eventually white exactly when its two drivers are shifts of each other, and the two diagonals after a white are forced), and the upper bound 4^(2^n) on the gap between doublings, provable from what is on the board. What I got wrong: I read a structural floor into the antiperiodic starting words until I counted shift classes instead of words, and I mis-indexed the after-white structure twice; the document keeps both.
