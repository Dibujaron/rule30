I was sent to move the P1 wall to the right edge, where the diagonals are exactly periodic and there are no transients to fight through. The geometry is as advertised and the conclusion is the opposite of the one hoped for.

The brief told me to check first whether the minimal period is itself a wall, and to stop if it was. It is not — I can pin it exactly at infinitely many explicit depths from a theorem already closed here, and the depths came back as Rowland's published sequence to the integer, all 27 values in range. But the premise underneath died instead, and harder. A centre column of period **four** produces a tower whose periods reach 2^27 by depth 64 with a doubling density of 0.406, against the seed's 2^27 at depth 64 and density 0.406. Identical to three digits. So `rightDiagonal_period_unbounded`, which is proved on this board, cannot imply P1, and neither can any sharpening of it. The reason is one sentence: index 0 of the tower is the free constant the recurrence does not fix, so the periods are exactly what is left after the centre column has been divided out. That is the same fence as crystal 69 on the left, reached from the other side.

What survived is worth having anyway. The board's `rightDiagonal_first_failure` throws away a witness; kept, it names the diagonal that fails, and a new lemma — the p-shifted tower has the same free bits, so it is the same tower — upgrades agreement at one index to full periodicity below that depth. Together they give the minimal period exactly at infinitely many depths, and as a corollary they reproduce a published theorem of Rowland's from the diagonal side rather than from his bijectivity argument.

And one entry on the obstructions file needs amending rather than adding to. Obstruction 7 says no universal argument can give right-diagonal minimality, with a counterexample pair of periodic words. Those pairs never occur as consecutive diagonals of any picture — not one of the 144 pairs of minimal period exactly 4 is reachable — and the law holds across every boundary in the family, 0 failures in 4.19 million transitions. So the universal induction that entry rules out is not ruled out; it is the thing to try.
