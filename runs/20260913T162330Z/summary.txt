Szego proved in 1922 that a power series with finitely many distinct coefficients is either a rational function or has the unit circle as a natural boundary, and Polya and Carlson proved the same dichotomy for integer coefficients with radius one; so P1 is exactly the statement that the unit circle is a natural boundary of F, and P2 is a statement about the radial behaviour of (1 minus x) times F(x) as x tends to 1, where Tauberian theorems of Karamata type say which boundary behaviour forces a density. Nobody on this board has looked at the column from the complex side; the earlier Dirichlet-series attempt died for wanting a closed form, which the power-series arm does not need.
