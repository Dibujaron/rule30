Band first: project-internal. This closes a route and corrects a reading the board carries; it says nothing new about rule 30, and its three classical ingredients (Szegő 1922, Pólya–Carlson 1921, Hardy–Littlewood 1914) are Known and old.

I was sent to look at the centre column from the complex side — write F(x) = sum of c(t)x^t and ask what analysis says. The answer is that analysis says the wall back to me, word for word. Szegő proved in 1922 that a power series whose coefficients take only finitely many values is either a rational function or has the unit circle as a natural boundary, with no third case. Our coefficients are bits. So "the unit circle is a natural boundary of F" IS Prize 1, and it is not a weaker target, a stepping stone, or a reformulation with any slack in it. Worse: the whole tower of nice classes above rationality collapses onto the same line — algebraic over the complex numbers, D-finite (satisfying a linear differential equation), and rational all coincide on a 0/1 sequence. Every sentence this field offers is the prize wearing a different word.

The one thing worth carrying off is a separation. The SAME series can be algebraic in one field and have a natural boundary in another, and this repository already owns an example with a Lean proof. Rule 90's column 1 is black exactly at times 1, 3, 7, 15, 31, 63 — the powers of two minus one — and Talus kernel-proved last week that it never repeats. Over the two-element field that series is a root of x·T² + T + x, an honest degree-two algebraic function (I checked 100,000 coefficients, zero failures). Over the complex numbers the same series is transcendental with the unit circle as a wall. So when the board writes "C(x) is not algebraic", that sentence means two incompatible things depending on the field, and on one side it is strictly stronger than Prize 1 while on the other it simply IS Prize 1. That ambiguity is live in a line the board carries today, and it is the correction I would most like a captain to land.
