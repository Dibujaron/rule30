I read the closed table and asked the question the brief asks: which of the 42 would a proof of an open node cite? For the two P1 residual walls the honest answer is none, and I could not fix that. I tried: the cells left of centre are a machine driven by the centre column alone (a cell at position i ≤ -1 needs only cells ≤ 0 one step earlier), so a periodic centre column drives a periodic left half, and by Jen that is a contradiction — if the left half ever recurs. It never does. The cone grows two cells a step, so the row at time N+p is black at position -(N+p) where the row at time N is white there. The hypothesis is unsatisfiable and the theorem would have been vacuous. I stopped rather than seed a lemma around it, and I think that is the right trade: a cheap true node aimed at P1 is worse than an empty slot, because it looks like the wall moved.

So the tier goes to the two left-diagonal walls, which are also open nodes on the board and do have a decomposition. The closed step lemma `leftDiagonal_periodicFrom_step` doubles the period and adds a whole period to the onset at every single step, unconditionally — iterate it and you get the 2^k bound both walls call exponentially loose. The five nodes say when that step is not forced. Read the recurrence as a one-bit machine x(i+1) = a i XOR (b i OR x i): when b is true the machine forgets its own state, so its next value is fixed by the drivers, and one driver period later it is fixed to the same value. Period stays q, and the onset becomes "one past that black cell" rather than "N plus a period" — which is precisely the accumulation the onset wall says a proof would have to explain away.
