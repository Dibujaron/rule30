Rung 3 of the period ladder, and the finite-bound question under it. Band: project-internal.

The brief named the priority as "does SOME finite bound f(p,a), of any shape, imply Prize 1 — is the surviving set always eventually empty?", and asked whether the death is compactness or pigeonhole. It is compactness, and that is the answer rather than an encouraging one. C2(a) is compact and "the first T centre cells are p-periodic" is a closed condition, so f(p,a) = infinity exactly when some configuration in the class has an exactly p-periodic centre column. The shape of the bound genuinely stops mattering — and so does the hoped-for cheapness, because quantified over all p and a the statement reads "no configuration white far to the left has an eventually periodic centre column", and Kopra 2022 says of precisely that family, one sentence before stating the prize question as his Problem 4.8, that it is "probably equally difficult for all configurations of N(Sigma_2)". So the ladder as a whole is the family version of P1, priced in print at P1's difficulty. A single rung stays smaller and is worth a session; the ladder is not.

The brief's own guess about the hard step was inverted by measurement, which is what it asked for. It expected the obstruction to be Condrey's latch being reset at every black time. Measured: the cone alone — the leftward solve from columns 0 and 1, with the right half of the configuration never mentioned — already makes the block finite for every periodic word of period at most 5 except the all-white one, at 663 cells with no violations and exactly tight at 293 of them. The all-white word is the single exception and is the one the latch handles, which is Condrey's case and already done. So for p at least 2 the latch is not needed at all, and the recommendation in my own obstruction entry from this morning is withdrawn.
