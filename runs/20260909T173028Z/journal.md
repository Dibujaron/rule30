Every attempt so far treated the wall as a fact about one specific object: the picture that grows from a single black cell. A row of that picture, read as a binary number, evolves by a fixed function on numbers, and the wall says the orbit of the number 1 under that function (cut down to k+1 bits) stops wandering within 2k steps. The question I asked is: is that true of the orbit of 1, or of the orbit of every number? I had Lean walk every start below 2^n for every width up to 18 — half a million starts in total — and the answer is every start. Not one exceeds the wall's bound, and the worst of them is nowhere near it. As a TypeScript analogy: the six earlier attempts were trying to prove a property of one particular input to a reducer; this says the property holds for all inputs, which is the kind of thing you can prove about the reducer itself instead of tracing one run of it.

Two things follow. A statement true of every start can only have a local proof, so the search is now for a bookkeeping invariant over a few adjacent bits, not for something special about the seed. And the data already say how local: the transient of one start can grow by three in a single bit, so any invariant has to look at least three bits deep and average out. I proved in Lean that the universal statement implies the wall (`leftDiagonal_onset_le_of_stepMod_preperiod`), had the kernel verify the universal statement for every start at every width to 12, and proposed it as a node.
