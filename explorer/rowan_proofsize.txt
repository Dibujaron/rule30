  Expect most control rules to have NO finite f(a), and that is the
  interesting part rather than a gap in the table.  Reading the rule
  rightwards along a row, each extra time step brings in exactly one
  new free cell of the initial row.  If the rule's output still depends
  on its right neighbour, that new cell can always be chosen to make
  the column come out however you like, so the block extends forever
  and the formula is satisfiable at every L.  Rules 90 and 150 XOR the
  right neighbour in, so they never lose that dependence.
  Rule 30's c(x-1) XOR (c(x) OR c(x+1)) does lose it: wherever the
  centre cell is already black the OR saturates and the right neighbour
  stops mattering.  That is why rule 30 has a finite f(a) at all, and
  it is the whole reason this family has an UNSAT instance for rule 30
